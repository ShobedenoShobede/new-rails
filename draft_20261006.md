**HEADLINE**  
U.S. regulators, credit‑rating firms and fintechs move to formalise crypto‑lending, custody and settlement as China’s peer‑to‑peer stablecoin activity surges  

---

### WHAT HAPPENED  

In the United States, two regulators are converging on a common regulatory scaffolding for digital‑asset activities. The Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) announced that, after Congress rejected the CLARITY market‑structure bill, it will rely on its existing statutory powers to shape a crypto‑framework, joining the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) in a coordinated proposal (CoinTelegraph, “CFTC joins SEC in proposing crypto framework”). At the same time, S&P Global released a risk‑assessment methodology for crypto‑lending vaults, covering liquidity, credit, operational, market, regulatory and technology risk. The model targets the roughly $10 billion of assets now flowing into these vaults (CoinTelegraph, “S&P Global brings risk assessments to growing crypto lending vault sector”).  

On the industry side, payments‑infrastructure firm Modern Treasury filed for a limited‑purpose national trust charter that would let it hold stablecoins and provide related fiat services under a U.S. trust‑bank licence (CoinTelegraph, “Modern Treasury seeks US trust bank charter”). A parallel move comes from OKX, which launched “OKX Money,” a mobile app that lets users hold, send and spend dollar‑backed stablecoins while earning up to 10 % annual percentage yield on qualifying balances (CoinTelegraph, “OKX eyes emerging markets with yield‑offering stablecoin savings and payments app”).  

Across the Pacific, Chainalysis data show that unique wallets used for peer‑to‑peer (P2P) stablecoin transfers in China grew 43‑fold between Q1 2024 and Q2 2026, despite the country’s strict crypto bans. The growth reflects a shift from exchange‑based trading to direct wallet‑to‑wallet payments (CoinTelegraph, “China P2P stablecoin wallets grew 43x despite crypto restrictions”). Finally, the Solana Foundation announced a delivery‑versus‑payment (DvP) protocol that settles asset transfers and payments in seconds, aimed at financial institutions that need fast, low‑cost settlement (CoinTelegraph, “Solana Foundation targets settlement in seconds with DvP launch”).  

---

### THE BIGGER PICTURE  

All of these moves are part of a single, emerging rail: the institutional‑grade infrastructure that can treat digital assets like any other cash‑equivalent. In the legacy system, banks and custodians control settlement, collateral and credit‑risk assessment through well‑tested processes that are heavily regulated. The new rail seeks to replicate those functions—risk rating, custody, settlement and yield generation—using blockchain‑native tools, but within a regulatory envelope that satisfies policymakers.  

Who stands to win?  
* **Traditional banks and trust companies** that secure a charter or partnership (e.g., Modern Treasury) can expand their service catalogue without building a blockchain stack from scratch, retaining fee income from custody and settlement.  
* **Institutional investors and treasury teams** gain a clearer risk signal from S&P’s framework, making it easier to allocate capital to crypto‑lending vaults or stablecoin‑based cash‑management products.  
* **Fintechs targeting emerging markets** (OKX Money) can attract retail deposits by offering yields that rival or exceed local bank rates, especially where conventional banking is under‑served.  

Who loses?  
* **Unregulated crypto‑lending platforms** that cannot meet the new rating standards may see capital flee to vetted vaults, compressing yields and forcing consolidation.  
* **Exchange‑centric trading models** in jurisdictions like China, where users are already moving to P2P wallets, will see further erosion as stablecoins become a de‑facto medium of exchange.  
* **Regulators who stay on the sidelines** risk being bypassed by private‑sector standards that could lock in market structures before legislation catches up.  

The old system relied on a centralized ledger (the Federal Reserve’s Fedwire, clearing houses, etc.) and a hierarchy of custodians. The new system layers a distributed ledger under that hierarchy, offering near‑instant settlement (Solana DvP), transparent collateral (stablecoins), and algorithmic risk scoring (S&P’s model). The coexistence of both rails is inevitable for now, but the speed, cost and data‑richness of the blockchain layer are creating pressure to migrate high‑value, low‑risk cash‑management functions onto it.  

---

### WHAT TO WATCH  

- **CFTC‑SEC joint rule proposal** – Expect a public comment period in the next 60 days, followed by a possible joint rule release before the end of 2026. The final language will dictate whether crypto‑lending vaults must register as investment companies or qualify for a new “digital‑asset custodian” exemption.  
- **Modern Treasury trust charter decision** – The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) typically takes 90–120 days to act. Approval would set a precedent for non‑bank fintechs to hold stablecoins under a federally chartered trust.  
- **S&P Global risk‑assessment adoption** – Watch for the first set of ratings published for major vaults (e.g., BlockFi, Nexo, Celsius). Institutional allocations could shift within weeks of those releases.  
- **OKX Money rollout in emerging markets** – Track user‑growth metrics in Southeast Asia and Latin America; regulatory responses in those regions (e.g., India’s RBI, Brazil’s CVM) could either accelerate or stall the product.  
- **Solana DvP integration by banks** – Pilot programs announced by at least two mid‑size U.S. banks are slated for Q4 2026. Successful pilots could trigger broader adoption and push legacy clearing houses to consider blockchain‑based settlement alternatives.  

These developments together signal that the “crypto‑banking” rail is moving from experiment to regulated utility. Stakeholders who can align their technology, compliance and risk‑management functions with this emerging framework will capture the next wave of digital‑asset cash‑management business.
