**Headline**  
Banks and Treasuries Confront a New Wave of Crypto Integration: Stable‑coin rollouts, real‑time on‑chain data, and emerging legal‑operational risks  

---

### What Happened  

In the past 24 hours a series of announcements have highlighted how the traditional financial ecosystem is being forced to accommodate crypto‑related services.  

* **Prediction‑market deposits go crypto‑native.** Underdog, a platform that lets users bet on real‑world events, has partnered with Zerohash to let eligible users fund their accounts with digital assets 24 hours a day. The service is slated to launch later this year and will be the first of its kind to accept crypto deposits for prediction‑market wagering (CoinTelegraph).  

* **On‑chain market data becomes “real‑time.”** Analytics firm Dune announced a data‑feed product that streams trade information from roughly 200 blockchain‑based venues into institutional systems. The feed is positioned as a bridge for banks and asset managers that need timely exposure to tokenized stocks, derivatives and other on‑chain assets as regulators begin to scrutinise these markets (CoinTelegraph).  

* **Stable‑coin cooperation spreads across Southeast Asia.** Singapore’s BLOOM initiative, a consortium aimed at standardising cross‑border stable‑coin usage, has added Malaysia and Thailand as participants. BLOOM’s goal is to reduce settlement costs and simplify compliance for banks that want to move tokenised deposits across the region (CoinTelegraph).  

* **HSBC rolls out a retail stable‑coin in Hong Kong.** The bank introduced “RedCoin,” a Hong Kong‑dollar‑backed token that can be used through its PayMe wallet for everyday payments. HSBC also signalled future plans to support tokenised investment subscriptions and to extend the token to institutional clients (CoinTelegraph).  

* **Ethereum delays a testnet upgrade.** The Glamsterdam “Hoodi” testnet, intended to experiment with new gas‑pricing rules, has been postponed after ChainSecurity identified 13 smart‑contract patterns that could be vulnerable under the proposed changes. Ethereum’s core developers are now working with affected projects on responsible disclosure (CoinTelegraph).  

* **Bitcoin ETFs see outflows.** U.S. spot Bitcoin exchange‑traded funds recorded net withdrawals of $635 million in the last three days, ending a brief period of inflows and leaving the sector with $175 million of net outflows for October (CoinTelegraph).  

* **Circle faces a shareholder lawsuit.** A Circle shareholder has filed a suit alleging that former President Donald Trump, his firm World Liberty, and real‑estate investor Steve Witkoff engaged in “corrupt” actions that left the company unable to compete with other stable‑coins, harming the value of USDC (CoinTelegraph).  

* **Self‑custody under scrutiny.** Recent hardware‑wallet hacks have reignited the debate over whether retail investors should hold crypto themselves. Critics argue that the complexity of multisignature and “seed‑less” wallets may outweigh the security benefits for beginners (CoinTelegraph).  

---

### The Bigger Picture  

All of these events point to a single, accelerating trend: **the financial‑services rail that once separated fiat‑based banking from decentralized finance is being rebuilt with new “track” that blends the two.**  

1. **Who is building the new rail?**  
   * **Banks and payment providers** are actively issuing their own stable‑coins (HSBC’s RedCoin) and joining regional consortia (BLOOM) to create interoperable token‑based settlement layers.  
   * **Crypto‑native platforms** such as Underdog are seeking to widen their user base by accepting fiat‑equivalent digital assets, effectively turning prediction markets into a new distribution channel for crypto liquidity.  
   * **Data‑infrastructure firms** like Dune are providing the “real‑time telemetry” that banks need to treat on‑chain trades with the same confidence they give to traditional exchanges.  

2. **Who stands to win?**  
   * **Treasury and cash‑management teams** that adopt tokenised deposits can lower cross‑border settlement times from days to minutes and reduce correspondent‑bank fees. The BLOOM expansion suggests a regional network effect that could make tokenised liquidity the cheapest way to move money between Singapore, Malaysia and Thailand.  
   * **Institutional investors** gain a richer data set (Dune’s feeds) and a broader set of investable products (prediction‑market exposure, tokenised stocks, Bitcoin ETFs). The ability to ingest on‑chain data into existing risk‑management systems lowers the barrier to entry for large‑scale allocation.  
   * **Banks that issue stable‑coins** can capture new retail and small‑business payment volume that would otherwise flow to fintech‑only wallets. HSBC’s RedCoin, linked to a widely used PayMe app, illustrates how a legacy bank can leverage an existing user base to pilot tokenised payments.  

3. **Who may lose or face new risk?**  
   * **Traditional settlement providers** could see a decline in wire‑transfer volumes as tokenised deposits become the default for cross‑border trade.  
   * **Banks that delay token‑coin adoption** may be left with legacy infrastructure that cannot meet the speed or cost expectations of corporate treasurers.  
   * **Crypto‑centric firms** face heightened operational risk. The Ethereum testnet delay shows that even well‑funded, open‑source projects can encounter security setbacks that ripple to downstream users. Similarly, the Circle lawsuit underscores how political and reputational risk can translate into tangible financial damage for stable‑coin issuers.  
   * **Retail investors** remain vulnerable. The renewed debate over self‑custody highlights that the “do‑it‑yourself” model may still be unsuitable for most non‑technical users, potentially slowing mass adoption unless custodial solutions improve.  

4. **What does this mean for the old system versus the new one?**  
   * The **old system**—centralised ledgers, overnight settlement, and a reliance on correspondent banking—continues to dominate large‑value, regulated flows but is increasingly being challenged on cost and speed.  
   * The **new system**—tokenised assets, real‑time on‑chain data, and decentralized settlement—offers speed, transparency and programmable features but brings new layers of regulatory, security and governance complexity. The coexistence of both rails is likely to persist for several years, with hybrid models (e.g., banks offering custodial stable‑coins) becoming the norm.
