**Headline**  
UK finance chiefs back tokenisation; US exchanges eye tokenised stocks while Asian‑US dollar settlement links and regulatory shifts reshape the crypto‑banking landscape  

---  

### What happened  

A Lloyds‑commissioned survey released on 1 October found that **71 % of senior finance executives in the United Kingdom expect tokenisation to fundamentally reshape financial services**.  Respondents cited faster payments and near‑instant settlement as the most compelling benefits, and the study notes that the UK is actively building the digital‑infrastructure needed to support tokenised assets. (​CoinTelegraph, “71 % of UK finance leaders expect tokenisation to reshape financial services”)  

In the United States, **OKX and the parent of the New York Stock Exchange (ICE) have filed a joint application to launch a platform that will offer tokenised shares of more than 60 US‑listed companies** under the SEC’s new “innovation exemption.”  The move signals a push to bring blockchain‑based securities to mainstream investors while staying within the regulator’s sandbox. (CoinTelegraph, “OKX, NYSE parent file to launch tokenized US stock platform”)  

Across the Atlantic, **Anchorage Digital, the $4.2 billion crypto‑bank, announced a 17 % reduction in staff** as it expands its institutional services, including a new stable‑coin issuance programme and a $100 million investment from Tether. (CoinTelegraph, “$4.2B crypto bank Anchorage Digital cuts 17 % of workforce”)  

In Europe, **Bitpanda’s co‑CEO Christian Trummer said the EU’s Markets in Crypto‑Assets (MiCA) framework has boosted retail confidence in regulated exchanges**, while calling for tougher enforcement against non‑compliant firms. (CoinTelegraph, “European crypto users have ‘more faith’ in regulated firms under MiCA”)  

Other notable moves include **Zcash hiring a Washington lobbyist to influence the CLARITY Act and two digital‑asset tax proposals**; **Payward (Kraken’s parent) adding a 24/7 US‑dollar settlement corridor with Singapore‑based Gulf Bank, enabling instant cross‑border dollar flows for select Asian and Gulf institutions**; and **former SEC chair Jay Clayton being appointed the U.S. administration’s AI czar, a development that has sparked speculative headlines about Bitcoin reaching $600 k by 2029**. (CoinTelegraph, various articles)  

Finally, **Republican gubernatorial hopeful Vivek Ramaswamy, a former presidential candidate with a personal stake in a digital‑asset manager, is deliberately down‑playing crypto in his Ohio campaign**, despite receiving significant industry donations. (CoinTelegraph, “Vivek Ramaswamy’s gubernatorial run lacks the crypto rhetoric of his presidential bid”)  

---  

### The bigger picture  

All of these signals point to a **rebuilding of the “settlement rail” that has underpinned banking and securities for decades**.  The old rail—paper certificates, batch‑processed clearing houses, and a reliance on correspondent banking for cross‑border dollars—is being supplemented, and in some cases bypassed, by **token‑based settlement layers that promise near‑real‑time finality**.  

The **winners** in this transition are likely to be **large, regulated institutions that can marry legacy compliance frameworks with blockchain infrastructure**.  Lloyds’ survey shows that senior finance leaders already view tokenisation as a competitive advantage, suggesting banks that invest early in token‑ready platforms will capture new fee streams from faster corporate payments, syndicated loans, and securities issuance.  The **OKX/ICE partnership** demonstrates that traditional exchange operators are willing to co‑develop tokenised products, positioning themselves as the custodians of a hybrid market where digital and paper securities coexist.  Similarly, **Payward’s 24/7 dollar settlement link** gives institutional clients in Asia and the Gulf a direct, on‑chain bridge to the U.S. dollar, reducing reliance on legacy correspondent networks that can be slow and costly.  

The **losers** are the **fragmented, unregulated crypto service providers that have traditionally thrived on speed but lacked robust compliance**.  Bitpanda’s comments on MiCA illustrate that retail users now prefer regulated venues, and the EU’s enforcement push will squeeze out “wild west” exchanges that cannot meet licensing, AML, and consumer‑protection standards.  Anchorage’s workforce cut, despite its growth in stable‑coin issuance, signals that **scale‑up in the crypto‑banking space will come with leaner, more specialised teams focused on regulated product lines rather than broad‑based retail services**.  

For the **old system**, the shift means **pressure on legacy clearing houses and correspondent banks to modernise or risk losing volume**.  The instant settlement offered by tokenised assets erodes the value proposition of traditional batch processing, which relies on time‑lagged risk management.  Conversely, the **new system**—a blend of regulated token platforms, on‑chain settlement, and AI‑enhanced compliance—offers **greater transparency, lower operational risk, and new revenue models** such as settlement‑as‑a‑service and token‑based liquidity provision.  

A secondary, but no less important, dynamic is the **political and regulatory backdrop**.  The appointment of former SEC chair Jay Clayton as the U.S. AI czar adds a layer of policy focus on the intersection of AI, data, and digital assets.  While the “$600 k Bitcoin” narrative is speculative, the underlying message is clear: **regulators are now confronting the convergence of AI, crypto, and traditional finance**, and the policy response will shape the speed and direction of adoption.  Ramaswamy’s low‑key crypto stance in Ohio suggests that **political capital for crypto advocacy may be shifting from the national to the state and sector‑specific level**, where industry groups will need to tailor their lobbying efforts.

### What to watch  

- **UK tokenisation roadmap** – The Bank of England’s “Project Aurora” (expected to release a detailed implementation plan by Q2 2027) will outline the technical standards and regulatory sandbox rules that will determine how quickly banks can issue and settle tokenised assets.  

- **SEC innovation exemption progress** – The SEC is expected to publish final guidance on the tokenised‑stock exemption by **mid‑December 2026**.  The scope of permissible tokens, disclosure requirements, and market‑maker obligations will dictate whether platforms like OKX/ICE can scale beyond a pilot phase.  

- **MiCA enforcement actions** – The European Commission’s “Digital Asset Supervision Unit” is slated to publish its first enforcement guidelines in **January 2027**.  Watch for penalties against non‑licensed exchanges; this will clarify the competitive landscape for European crypto firms.  

- **Payward‑Gulf Bank settlement usage metrics** – The partnership aims to process **$5 billion in dollar transactions in its first 12 months**.  Quarterly volume reports, due in **Q1 2027**, will reveal whether institutional clients are shifting away from traditional correspondent banks.  

- **Political funding and lobbying trends** – Track the **2026‑2027 election cycles** for any uptick in crypto‑related campaign contributions or lobbying disclosures, especially in swing states like Ohio and Florida, where industry influence could affect state‑level regulatory approaches.
