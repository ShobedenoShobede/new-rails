**Headline**  
Corporate governance, ETF mechanics, and blockchain‑enabled settlement converge as banks, regulators, and asset managers reshape digital‑asset finance  

---

### What Happened  

In the past 24 hours a cluster of seemingly unrelated stories revealed a coordinated shift in how traditional finance is adapting to crypto‑related products.  

* **Executive equity dilution under fire** – VanEck, a major asset manager, publicly criticized Metaplanet for maintaining an executive share pool that remains “well above its digital‑asset treasury peers” even after the company reduced the pool by 41 %. VanEck’s comments highlight growing scrutiny of governance standards among firms that hold or issue crypto‑related securities (CoinTelegraph).  

* **Grayscale’s Zcash ETF prepares a forward split** – Grayscale Investments filed with the SEC for a 3‑for‑1 forward share split of its Zcash ETF, slated to take effect at the close of trading on 28 September. Existing shareholders will receive two additional shares for each share they own, a move intended to improve liquidity and price accessibility (CoinTelegraph).  

* **Tokenized bonds and same‑day settlement** – Hana Bank announced the issuance of a $100 million five‑year digital bond using Euroclear’s blockchain infrastructure. The platform reportedly cut settlement time from the traditional three‑to‑five business days to same‑day processing (CoinTelegraph).  

* **24‑hour won settlement pilot** – The Bank of Korea launched a pilot that allows foreign investors to settle South Korean won transactions in real‑time during their home‑country business hours. The network leverages a private‑ledger system to extend settlement windows beyond the local market’s daylight schedule (CoinTelegraph).  

* **ECB’s Pontes platform expands** – The European Central Bank introduced Pontes, a token‑settlement system that operates without stablecoins. Pontes will gradually increase operating hours and participant scope, with a full rollout targeted for 2028 (CoinTelegraph).  

* **Regulatory uncertainty around the CLARITY Act** – The U.S. Senate’s CLARITY Act, which would have provided a clear regulatory pathway for tokenized securities, stalled. Coinbase found itself in the spotlight as the SEC pressed ahead with separate token‑stock initiatives, while the broader crypto industry pushed deeper into payments (CoinTelegraph).  

* **Bitcoin’s price rally** – Bitcoin breached the $86 000 level for the first time since January, driven by falling oil prices and renewed risk appetite in U.S. equities markets (CoinTelegraph).  

Collectively, these items illustrate three intertwined trends: heightened governance expectations for crypto‑exposed firms, the maturation of ETF and token‑settlement mechanics, and the regulatory tug‑of‑war that continues to shape market participation.  

---

### The Bigger Picture  

**Rebuilding the “rail” of settlement and custody**  
The core infrastructure that moves value—settlement, custody, and reporting—has long been dominated by legacy clearing houses and custodians. The blockchain‑based bond issuance by Hana Bank and the Bank of Korea’s 24‑hour won pilot demonstrate that banks are now testing a parallel rail that can settle same‑day, cross‑border, and outside traditional market hours. The ECB’s Pontes platform adds a European counterpart that eliminates the need for stablecoins, focusing instead on central‑bank money as the settlement token.  

**Winners and losers**  
*Winners*:  
- **Banks that master hybrid models** – Institutions that can integrate private‑ledger settlement with existing core banking systems stand to capture new fee income and deepen relationships with corporate treasuries seeking faster funding.  
- **Asset managers that align governance with market expectations** – VanEck’s critique of Metaplanet signals that investors are willing to penalize firms with opaque or overly generous executive equity structures. Managers that adopt transparent compensation frameworks will likely enjoy lower cost‑of‑capital and smoother regulatory reviews.  

*Losers*:  
- **Legacy custodians and clearing houses that cling to batch‑oriented processes** – Same‑day, real‑time settlement erodes the value proposition of multi‑day clearing cycles, pressuring incumbents to modernize or lose market share.  
- **Crypto firms that rely on uncertain regulatory pathways** – The stalled CLARITY Act leaves tokenized securities in a limbo where only firms with strong compliance resources can continue to issue and list assets. Coinbase’s heightened scrutiny underscores the risk of operating without clear statutory backing.  

**Implications for the old vs. the new system**  
The old system is built on periodic settlement, centralized custodianship, and a regulatory framework that treats crypto as a peripheral asset class. The new system being assembled is a hybrid: it retains central‑bank money and regulated intermediaries but uses distributed ledger technology (DLT) to compress settlement cycles and expand operating windows. This hybrid model reduces counterparty risk, frees up capital tied up in settlement lag, and opens the door for a broader set of participants—including foreign investors—to access markets in real time.  

However, the transition is not seamless. Governance concerns, as highlighted by VanEck, show that the “digital‑asset treasury” label is not a free pass to lax oversight. Regulators are still calibrating how tokenized securities fit within existing securities laws, and the CLARITY Act’s delay signals that the United States may continue to rely on piecemeal guidance rather than a comprehensive framework. The net effect is a market where operational efficiency is improving faster than the certainty of the legal environment, creating a strategic dilemma for treasury teams and institutional investors: adopt the faster rails now and manage regulatory risk, or wait for clearer rules at the cost of slower funding.
