**Headline**  
Token‑Based Securities Move Forward While U.S. Regulators Remain a Bottleneck – Robinhood, Mirae, DTCC and Google Cloud Highlight the Split Between Innovation and Uncertainty  

---

### What Happened  

In the past 24 hours a cluster of announcements underscored how quickly the token‑based securities ecosystem is expanding, even as regulatory clarity in the United States lags behind.  

* **Robinhood’s warning** – CEO Vlad Tenev told investors that the temporary SEC exemption for tokenised equities is not enough for “serious assets” to thrive. He argued that without a durable regulatory framework, institutional players will stay on the sidelines (CoinTelegraph).  

* **Mirae Asset’s tokenised notes** – The Hong‑Kong arm of Mirae Asset Securities issued six‑month, $3 billion‑program notes on the Solana blockchain, marking the first time the firm has used a public‑layer protocol to distribute debt to institutional investors (CoinTelegraph).  

* **Euronext halts CoinShares Zcash ETP** – The French‑Dutch exchange paused the launch of a new exchange‑traded product that would give investors exposure to Zcash. The suspension is tied to “operational settlement requirements” that the central securities depository (CSD) has not yet been able to support (CoinTelegraph).  

* **DTCC’s tokenisation service** – The Depository Trust & Clearing Corporation’s subsidiary announced a post‑trade tokenisation platform that promises a “practical path” for moving existing securities onto a blockchain‑based ledger, positioning the DTCC as a bridge between legacy markets and distributed‑ledger technology (CoinTelegraph).  

* **Google Cloud’s MPC custody preview** – Google Cloud detailed a multi‑party computation (MPC) service designed for institutional Bitcoin custody, unveiled at a New York symposium on digital‑asset security. The offering aims to reduce single‑point‑of‑failure risk while meeting the operational demands of large custodians (CoinTelegraph).  

* **Legal and regulatory side‑bars** – In Canada, the criminal trial of Aiden Pleterski – dubbed the country’s “crypto king” – is moving toward a jury decision, keeping the spotlight on fraud enforcement in the digital‑asset space (CoinTelegraph). Meanwhile, CFTC Chair Mike Selig warned that autonomous AI agents could soon execute trades and launch tokens without human oversight, urging regulators to adapt rules that were written for people, not code (CoinTelegraph).  

* **Market snapshot** – A daily roundup from CoinTelegraph highlighted modest Bitcoin price movement, continued pressure on DeFi protocols, and a surge of regulatory filings across the U.S. and Europe, reinforcing the view that the sector remains volatile but increasingly mainstream.

### The Bigger Picture  

These headlines are not isolated anecdotes; they reveal a single, accelerating rail being rebuilt: the **post‑trade lifecycle for securities** – issuance, settlement, custody and reporting – is being re‑engineered on distributed‑ledger technology (DLT).  

1. **Who is rebuilding the rail?**  
   * **Infrastructure incumbents** – DTCC’s entry signals that the traditional clearinghouse is willing to embed tokenisation into its core workflow rather than watching it develop on the fringe. By offering a “practical path,” the DTCC is attempting to standardise token formats, settlement timing and custody interfaces, effectively translating the speed and transparency of blockchain into the language of existing market participants.  
   * **Fintech platforms and crypto‑native firms** – Robinhood, CoinShares and Mirae Asset are pushing the front‑end of the rail, creating market‑ready products that demand a token‑enabled back‑office. Their willingness to launch tokenised equities, Zcash ETPs and Solana‑based notes shows confidence that the demand side is maturing.  
   * **Cloud and security providers** – Google Cloud’s MPC service addresses a critical bottleneck: secure, scalable custody. By moving the cryptographic heavy‑lifting to a cloud environment, the service reduces the need for bespoke hardware security modules, making institutional Bitcoin storage more palatable for banks and treasury desks.  

2. **Who wins?**  
   * **Institutional investors** – The ability to receive debt or equity on a blockchain reduces settlement cycles from T+2/3 to near‑instantaneous, freeing up capital and lowering counter‑party risk. Tokenised notes on Solana, for example, give investors exposure to high‑yield, short‑duration instruments while preserving the audit trail required by regulators.  
   * **Banks and custodians that adapt early** – Firms that integrate MPC custody or partner with DTCC’s token platform can offer a differentiated service set, attracting asset managers looking for both speed and security.  
   * **Technology vendors** – Cloud providers and DLT platforms stand to capture recurring revenue from licensing, infrastructure and professional services as the token rail expands.  

3. **Who loses?**  
   * **Legacy custodians that cling to air‑gapped hardware** – Those that cannot demonstrate comparable security or settlement speed may see asset flows shift to cloud‑based MPC custodians.  
   * **Regulatory‑driven market makers** – In the United States, the lack of a clear, permanent token‑security framework discourages market‑making activity. Robinhood’s warning underscores that without SEC‑backed certainty, liquidity providers will stay out, leaving tokenised equities thinly traded.  
   * **CSDs that cannot upgrade** – The Euronext pause of the Zcash ETP illustrates that central securities depositories must upgrade settlement infrastructure to handle token‑based settlement. Until they do, product launches will be delayed, harming both issuers and investors.  

4. **Old system vs. new system** – The traditional pipeline—underwritten issuance, manual clearing, custodial safekeeping, and delayed settlement—relies on a series of bilateral reconciliations and physical paperwork. The emerging token rail collapses many of those steps into a single, immutable ledger entry, delivering real‑time finality and programmable compliance (e.g., automated corporate actions). However, the transition is not seamless: regulatory approval, interoperability standards and operational risk controls must be aligned. The current “split‑screen” where fintech innovators push forward while regulators and legacy CSDs lag creates a temporary friction point that could shape market structure for the next 3‑5 years.
