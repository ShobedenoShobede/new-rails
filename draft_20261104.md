**HEADLINE**  
U.S. political shift and European budget delay reshape crypto‑tax, custody and tokenisation outlook  

**WHAT HAPPENED**  
In the past 24 hours a cluster of policy and market signals has emerged from both sides of the Atlantic. In France, the National Assembly postponed the vote on the 2027 budget’s revenue chapter for a second time, effectively shelving a proposed “crypto exit‑tax” that would have taxed residents on the unrealised gains of crypto assets when they moved abroad. The delay pushes any decision on the tax to after the November 17 deadline for the first reading of the budget (CoinTelegraph).  

Across the Atlantic, the U.S. midterm elections produced a decisive change in congressional control. Democrats now hold a majority in the Senate and have secured enough seats in the House to claim overall control of Congress (CoinTelegraph). The new alignment is expected to revive legislative initiatives such as the CLARITY Act, which would impose stricter reporting and anti‑money‑laundering requirements on digital‑asset service providers. At the same time, senior executives from the decentralized‑exchange protocol 1inch warned that, regardless of the election outcome, U.S. regulatory agencies—particularly the SEC and the CFTC—will continue to drive crypto policy (CoinTelegraph).  

Market reaction has been muted but telling. Bitcoin held just above its $76 000 low as equity markets priced in “political gridlock” and uncertainty about future fiscal and regulatory moves (CoinTelegraph). Meanwhile, a high‑profile token‑listing dispute resurfaced when SpaceX, along with several other issuers, formally objected to the listing of tokenised shares of its stock on the offshore platform OKXICE, citing concerns over investor protection and jurisdiction (CoinTelegraph). Finally, Grayscale’s chief investment officer, Zach Pandl, down‑played the impact of the recent $6.3 billion Binance hack, calling it a “temporary setback” and emphasizing that institutional custodians are strengthening safeguards to protect large‑scale holdings (CoinTelegraph).  

**THE BIGGER PICTURE**  
These events collectively signal a re‑balancing of the “old” financial order—characterised by sovereign tax regimes, centralized exchanges and legacy custodians—and the “new” digital‑asset ecosystem that seeks broader access, tokenised securities and cross‑border fluidity. The French budget postponement illustrates the political sensitivity of taxing crypto holdings. By shelving the exit‑tax, France is temporarily preserving the attractiveness of its market for crypto‑savvy investors and firms, but it also leaves a legislative gap that could be filled later with a more aggressive stance. The short‑term winner is the crypto industry operating in Europe, which avoids an immediate compliance shock; the longer‑term loser could be the French treasury, which forgoes a potential new revenue stream.  

In the United States, the Democratic takeover of Congress is likely to accelerate the push for tighter reporting and consumer‑protection rules. The CLARITY Act, if passed, would embed crypto firms more firmly within the existing securities‑regulation framework, effectively pulling tokenised securities like those attempted on OKXICE back under U.S. oversight. SpaceX’s objection to the OKXICE listing is a concrete example of issuers pre‑emptively protecting their brand and shareholder rights against offshore tokenisation that operates outside U.S. securities law. The winners here are traditional market participants—broker‑dealers, custodians and regulated exchanges—who stand to retain their gate‑keeping role. The losers are innovators seeking to bypass domestic regulation via offshore tokenised securities, and investors who could lose access to a broader set of tradable assets.  

Bitcoin’s price resilience amid political uncertainty underscores the asset’s evolving role as a “risk‑off” hedge for some institutional portfolios, but also highlights its sensitivity to macro‑policy cues. The market’s calm suggests that professional investors are already pricing in the possibility of tighter U.S. regulation and the delayed French tax, preferring to wait for clearer guidance before reallocating capital. Meanwhile, the Binance hack reminder, coupled with Pandl’s reassurance, reinforces a growing consensus that institutional custody standards are maturing. Custodians that can demonstrate robust, multi‑layer security and clear incident‑response protocols will likely capture a larger share of the $10‑plus trillion digital‑asset custody market that is projected to develop over the next five years.  

Overall, the “rail” being rebuilt is the regulatory and compliance infrastructure that will determine how digital assets interact with traditional finance. The side that can align technology, legal frameworks and risk management—particularly in the U.S. and Europe—will shape the next wave of institutional participation. Companies that adapt quickly to the emerging U.S. legislative agenda, maintain strong custody practices, and respect jurisdictional limits on tokenised securities are positioned to win. Those that rely on offshore tokenisation or assume a regulatory‑free environment risk being sidelined or forced into costly compliance retrofits.
