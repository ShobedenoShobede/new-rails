**Headline**  
Hyperliquid pushes retail access to Wall‑Street‑style perpetual contracts while regulators tighten stable‑coin rules and banks roll out institutional crypto custody – a clash of democratization and compliance in the next‑gen finance ecosystem.  

---  

### What Happened  

**Retail‑focused perpetual contracts** – Hyperliquid’s chief executive told *CoinTelegraph* that the traditional Wall Street model of generating wealth through leveraged derivatives is “unsustainable for most participants.” The firm is betting that its zero‑fee, high‑leverage perpetual contracts can give retail traders the same risk‑return profile that hedge funds enjoy on equity and futures markets. Hyperliquid says its on‑chain order‑book and deep liquidity pool are designed to keep slippage low even for large positions, a claim it plans to prove by expanding into the U.S. futures‑grade market later this year.  

**Stable‑coin and token‑asset pilots in Central Asia** – Tether and the National Bank of Kazakhstan announced a joint feasibility study for a tenge‑backed stablecoin and a broader token‑isation framework for real‑world assets, *CoinTelegraph* reported. The pilot aims to create a regulated digital currency that can be used for cross‑border payments and to digitise commodities such as gold or grain. Both parties stress that the project will be conducted under existing Kazakh AML/CFT rules, with a view to a sandbox‑type launch before the end of 2027.  

**Speed‑record for off‑chain tunnels** – The Sui blockchain disclosed that its “off‑chain tunnels,” a programmable layer that pushes high‑frequency AI agent traffic off the main chain, achieved 40.6 million transactions per second (TPS) in a live stress test. The test simulated thousands of autonomous trading bots interacting with DeFi protocols, showing that Sui can sustain massive throughput without sacrificing finality.  

**Enterprise stable‑coin payments via SAP** – Circle announced a partnership with Tereina, a SAP‑backed fintech, to embed USDC and EURC payments directly into SAP’s ERP suite. Corporate finance teams will be able to initiate, reconcile and settle stable‑coin transactions without leaving the familiar SAP interface, a move that could accelerate stable‑coin adoption in supply‑chain finance.  

**Regulatory tightening in the EU** – ESMA gave crypto firms a three‑month deadline to cease offering “non‑MiCA‑compliant” stablecoins, according to *CoinTelegraph*. The regulator warned that continued exposure could trigger enforcement actions, signalling the EU’s resolve to enforce the new Markets in Crypto‑Assets (MiCA) framework and to protect investors from unbacked or opaque tokens.  

**Institutional custody expansion in Asia** – Standard Chartered disclosed plans to launch a crypto‑custody platform in Singapore, covering cryptocurrencies, stablecoins and tokenised assets for eligible corporate and institutional clients. The service will be built on a combination of the bank’s existing custody infrastructure and third‑party blockchain‑node providers, aiming for a Q2‑2027 go‑live.

### The Bigger Picture  

All of these stories are different strands of the same transformation: the **re‑building of the “wealth‑creation rail” that has historically been the exclusive domain of banks, broker‑dealers and large‑cap hedge funds**.  

1. **Who is rebuilding the rail?**  
   *Fintech platforms* like Hyperliquid and Circle are laying new tracks that run on public blockchains, offering zero‑fee, high‑leverage products and instant stable‑coin settlement directly inside enterprise software. Simultaneously, *legacy banks* such as Standard Chartered are retrofitting their vaults and compliance engines to accommodate the same digital assets, effectively becoming the “track‑owners” for institutional traffic. Regulators, represented by ESMA and the Kazakh central bank, are acting as the signal controllers, dictating which trains can run on which lines.  

2. **Who wins?**  
   - **Retail and mid‑size corporates** gain a direct line to market‑making tools that were previously gated behind high minimums and costly clearing houses. Hyperliquid’s perpetual contracts and Circle’s SAP‑embedded stable‑coin payments could dramatically lower transaction costs and execution latency for these users.  
   - **Banks that move quickly** stand to capture custody fees, cross‑border FX spreads and the ancillary services (e.g., compliance reporting, insurance) that accompany large‑scale digital‑asset holdings. Standard Chartered’s Singapore launch positions it to serve Asian corporates that are already experimenting with tokenised trade finance.  
   - **Stable‑coin issuers that obtain regulatory clarity** (e.g., Tether’s tenge pilot) will benefit from a sovereign‑backed legitimacy boost, opening doors to government‑to‑government payments and central‑bank digital currency (CBDC) interoperability.  

3. **Who loses?**  
   - **Traditional brokerage and clearing houses** risk losing volume as on‑chain perpetual contracts bypass their fee structures. Their relevance will shrink unless they can integrate with or provide similar high‑speed, low‑cost services.  
   - **Crypto projects that ignore compliance** will face market exclusion in the EU and potentially elsewhere, as the MiCA framework becomes the de‑facto baseline for stable‑coin legitimacy. The ESMA deadline forces a rapid purge of opaque tokens, which could also affect smaller DeFi protocols that rely on “algorithmic” stablecoins.  
   - **Banks that delay entry** risk being sidelined by fintechs that already own the technology stack and customer relationships, especially in fast‑moving markets like Singapore and Kazakhstan.  

4. **Old system vs. new system**  
   The legacy system is built on **centralised clearing, settlement windows of one to three days, and high intermediation fees**. The emerging system promises **near‑instant settlement, programmable money, and open‑access markets**. However, the new rail still needs the safety nets that the old system provides—robust custody, AML/KYC compliance, and legal certainty. The convergence we see—banks offering custody, fintechs offering regulated stablecoins, and regulators tightening the rulebook—suggests a hybrid model will dominate: **public‑chain infrastructure paired with institutional‑grade compliance layers**.

These developments together sketch a near‑term roadmap where **digital‑asset products move from niche experiments to core components of corporate treasury and retail investment strategies**. The firms that can blend open‑chain speed with compliance rigor are poised to dominate the next wave of financial intermediation.
