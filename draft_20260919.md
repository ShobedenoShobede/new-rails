**HEADLINE**  
U.S. single‑stock futures, EU MiCA expansion, and tokenized money‑market funds signal a rapid shift from legacy banking to hybrid crypto‑enabled finance  

---

### WHAT HAPPENED  

- **OG.com cleared for U.S. single‑stock futures** – Crypto.com’s sister exchange, OG.com, received conditional approval from the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission to list perpetual futures on individual equities. The SEC’s decision, announced by Crypto.com CEO Kris Marszalek, makes OG.com the first crypto‑focused platform to offer U.S. investors a bridge between traditional equity exposure and on‑chain perpetual contracts. (CoinTelegraph)

- **CLARITY Act stalls in the Senate** – The bipartisan “Crypto‑Linked Asset‑Based Regulation and Innovation” (CLARITY) Act failed a key vote in the Senate. Democrats are demanding additional consumer‑protection language, and the bill’s sponsors have only a narrow window before the current congressional session ends. (CoinTelegraph)

- **WisdomTree and MoonPay tokenise a $1.2 bn money‑market fund** – WisdomTree’s WTGXX fund will be issued as a blockchain‑native token. MoonPay plans to hold the token as part of its stablecoin‑reserve strategy and to use the tokenised vehicle to give U.S. retail investors easier access to a regulated money‑market product. (CoinTelegraph)

- **World launches a self‑custodial “super app”** – World Money, a self‑custodial wallet that bundles stablecoin payments, digital‑asset rewards, and on‑chain trading, has gone live in more than 150 jurisdictions. The app is positioned as a “one‑stop shop” for consumers who want to move money without a traditional bank. (CoinTelegraph)

- **Binance rebuffs MiCA speculation** – Amid rumors that European Central Bank President Christine Lagarde had intervened in Binance’s Greek MiCA application, the exchange said the reports were inaccurate and reiterated its commitment to securing a full EU operating licence. (CoinTelegraph)

- **dtcpay raises $25 m from SBI Group** – The Japanese‑backed stablecoin payments platform closed a Series A round led by SBI, earmarked for expanding its merchant network and building additional payment products. (CoinTelegraph)

- **HYPE token hits $90+ after Hyperliquid adds manual borrowing** – Hyperliquid introduced a manual borrowing feature that lets users take stablecoins against HYPE or Bitcoin collateral. The new product drove the HYPE token to an all‑time high of $90.92. (CoinTelegraph)

- **Banks double their presence on the EU MiCA provider register** – According to ESMA data, banks now account for roughly 23 % of the entities listed on the MiCA register, up from about 12 % in late June. The growth reflects a concerted push by traditional lenders to enter the regulated crypto‑service market in Europe. (CoinTelegraph)

### THE BIGGER PICTURE  

**Rebuilding the “rails” between capital markets and digital assets**  
All eight stories revolve around a single trend: the financial‑services “rails” that once moved only cash, securities, and payments through banks are being retrofitted to carry tokenised assets and on‑chain contracts. OG.com’s approval to list single‑stock perpetual futures is a concrete example of that retrofit in the United States. By allowing a crypto exchange to host equity‑linked derivatives, regulators are acknowledging that the same market‑making and clearing functions performed by CME or ICE can be replicated on a distributed‑ledger platform, provided the appropriate safeguards are in place.

In Europe, the MiCA framework is doing the same work on the supply side. The surge in bank participation on the ESMA register shows that legacy institutions are no longer content to watch from the sidelines. They are applying for licences that let them offer custodial wallets, token‑issuance services, and even crypto‑exchange operations under a single regulatory umbrella. Binance’s public denial of ECB interference underscores how high‑profile the race has become: the market is watching for the first major crypto‑exchange to secure a full EU licence.

**Winners and losers in the emerging hybrid ecosystem**  

- **Winners** –  
  1. **Tech‑forward banks and payment groups** (e.g., SBI, traditional banks entering MiCA) gain new revenue streams by packaging tokenised products for retail and corporate clients.  
  2. **Crypto‑native platforms that secure regulatory clearance** (OG.com, Binance, Hyperliquid) can attract institutional capital that previously stayed out of the space due to compliance risk.  
  3. **Asset managers that tokenise existing funds** (WisdomTree) unlock liquidity and broaden distribution, especially among younger investors who prefer on‑chain access.  

- **Losers** –  
  1. **Legacy payment rails that remain closed to crypto** (e.g., U.S. ACH networks) risk marginalisation as merchants and consumers adopt stablecoin‑based solutions like dtcpay.  
  2. **Regulatory proposals that stall** (the CLARITY Act) may leave a gap in U.S. policy that could be filled by state‑level or foreign regulations, creating a fragmented compliance landscape.  
  3. **Pure‑play crypto exchanges without a clear regulatory pathway** could see market share erode if banks and hybrid platforms capture the bulk of institutional demand.

**Old system vs. new system** – The traditional banking model relies on a centralized ledger, a single point of custody, and a regulated clearinghouse. The emerging model distributes custody (self‑custodial wallets like World Money), spreads clearing across smart contracts (perpetual futures on OG.com), and uses tokenised securities to bypass legacy depositories. The old system still holds the advantage of scale, brand trust, and established compliance infrastructure, but its advantage is eroding as regulators grant conditional approvals and as banks themselves become “crypto providers.” The new system’s advantage is speed, programmability, and access to a global, 24/7 market. The convergence we are witnessing suggests a future where the two rails operate side‑by‑side, with cross‑connectivity points (e.g., tokenised money‑market funds used as stablecoin reserves) becoming the norm rather than the exception.

### WHAT TO WATCH  

- **U.S. SEC final rulings on OG.com’s futures** – The SEC has granted conditional approval; a full licence or any subsequent restrictions are expected within the next 60 days. The outcome will set a precedent for other crypto exchanges seeking equity‑linked products.  

- **Legislative fate of the CLARITY Act** – A Senate amendment deadline is set for **October 15, 2026**. If the bill fails again, expect a shift toward state‑level crypto‑regulation or a push for a new federal framework.  

- **Regulatory status of tokenised money‑market funds** – The SEC and the Federal Reserve are reviewing the tokenisation of regulated funds. Watch for any guidance or enforcement actions in the **Q4 2026** filing season, especially concerning the use of such tokens as stablecoin collateral.

The composition of that list—particularly the proportion of banks vs. pure‑play crypto firms—will indicate how quickly the European market is moving toward a hybrid model.
