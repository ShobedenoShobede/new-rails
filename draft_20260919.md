**Headline**  
U.S. “CLARITY” bill stalls, while tokenized money‑market funds, bank‑driven MiCA listings and new self‑custody apps accelerate the shift from legacy banking to a hybrid crypto‑finance ecosystem.  

---

### What happened  

**U.S. legislative setback** – The “Crypto‑Linked Asset‑Recovery and Transparency (CLARITY) Act,” which would have created a federal framework for tracing and recovering illicit crypto assets, missed a crucial Senate vote.  According to *CoinTelegraph*, Democrats are still demanding amendments, and the window for passage is closing fast.  The bill is not dead, but its future is uncertain.  

**Tokenized money‑market fund expands** – WisdomTree’s $1.2 bn WTGXX tokenized money‑market fund is now being used by MoonPay to back its stablecoin reserves.  The partnership, reported by *CoinTelegraph*, is aimed at giving U.S. retail investors broader exposure to a regulated, yield‑producing crypto product.  

**World Money’s self‑custody super‑app** – The fintech startup World launched a self‑custodial “super app” that bundles stablecoin payments, digital‑asset rewards and on‑ramp trading in more than 150 countries.  *CoinTelegraph* notes the app is positioned as a one‑stop shop for consumers who want to hold and spend crypto without a traditional bank intermediary.  

**Binance’s MiCA stance** – Binance brushed off speculation that European Central Bank chief Christine Lagarde was intervening in its Greek MiCA (Markets in Crypto‑Assets) licensing bid.  The exchange reaffirmed its commitment to obtain a European licence, *CoinTelegraph* says.  

**dtcpay’s $25 m Series A** – Japan’s SBI Group led a $25 million financing round for stablecoin payments platform dtcpay.  The funds will be used to grow its merchant network and develop new payment products, according to *CoinTelegraph*.  

**HYPE token spikes** – The HYPE token reached an all‑time high of $90.92 after Hyperliquid introduced manual borrowing, allowing users to leverage stablecoins against HYPE and Bitcoin collateral.  *CoinTelegraph* reported the move pushed the token to a record level.  

**Banks on the EU MiCA register** – Banks now account for roughly 23 % of providers listed on the European Securities and Markets Authority’s MiCA register, up from about 12 % in June.  *CoinTelegraph* attributes the rise to a wave of traditional institutions seeking regulated crypto‑service licences.  

**Bitcoin rebounds** – Bitcoin climbed to $81,000 as U.S. 30‑year Treasury yields rose amid renewed oil‑price concerns.  The price move, covered by *CoinTelegraph*, underscores the cryptocurrency’s continued sensitivity to macro‑financial variables.

### The bigger picture  

All of these headlines are different facets of a single, accelerating rail‑rebuilding project: the convergence of regulated finance and crypto‑native services.  In the United States, the stalled CLARITY Act illustrates how legislative inertia can slow the creation of a clear, enforceable framework for digital assets.  The bill’s failure to pass means that, for now, law‑enforcement and compliance teams must continue to rely on a patchwork of existing AML/CTF rules, leaving a gap that private actors are eager to fill.  

In parallel, tokenized money‑market funds such as WisdomTree’s WTGXX are providing a bridge between traditional cash‑equivalent products and the crypto ecosystem.  By using a regulated, audited fund as reserve backing, MoonPay is giving stablecoin issuers a defensible source of liquidity that can satisfy both regulators and risk‑averse investors.  This model is likely to become a template for other “crypto‑linked” products, blurring the line between bank deposits and digital tokens.  

European banks are moving from “watchers” to “providers” on the MiCA register, effectively rewriting the supply‑side of the market.  Their growing share—now one in four crypto‑service providers—means that legacy institutions are positioning themselves to capture the same client base that fintechs and exchanges have been courting.  For banks, the upside is access to high‑margin crypto‑custody and payment services; the downside is the operational and compliance burden of meeting EU‑wide standards.  

The launch of World Money’s self‑custodial super‑app and dtcpay’s SBI‑backed expansion show how consumer‑facing products are being built to operate outside traditional bank accounts.  By giving users direct control over private keys while still offering familiar payment flows, these platforms threaten the “bank‑as‑intermediary” model, especially in emerging markets where banking penetration is low.  At the same time, Binance’s insistence on securing a MiCA licence signals that even the largest crypto exchanges recognize the long‑term value of regulatory legitimacy in Europe.  

Finally, market‑price signals—Bitcoin’s rebound and HYPE’s record rally—remind strategists that macro forces still dominate crypto valuation.  Higher Treasury yields and oil‑price shocks are driving risk‑on and risk‑off flows, affecting both legacy assets and digital ones.  The fact that a leveraged token like HYPE can surge on a borrowing feature launch underscores the growing sophistication (and risk) of crypto‑based credit products.  

**Winners:**  
* Traditional banks that secure MiCA licences and launch regulated crypto services—capturing fee income and client stickiness.  
* Asset managers that tokenise existing funds, providing compliant yield products for crypto users.  
* Fintech platforms that combine self‑custody with fiat on‑ramps, expanding financial inclusion.  

**Losers:**  
* Crypto projects that rely on ambiguous regulatory environments—risking exclusion from mainstream markets.  
* Legacy payment processors that fail to integrate stablecoins or tokenized assets, losing relevance to merchants.  
* Investors who overlook the macro‑risk linkages between bond yields, commodity shocks and crypto prices.  

Overall, the old system—characterised by siloed banking, fiat‑only payments and slow legislative change—is being reshaped into a hybrid architecture where regulated crypto products, self‑custody apps and bank‑driven licences coexist.  The balance of power is shifting toward institutions that can navigate both worlds.
