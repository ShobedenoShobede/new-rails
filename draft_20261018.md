**Headline**  
Binance.US eyes $1 billion valuation, while Deutsche Bank rolls out Asian crypto custody and stablecoins slash cross‑border costs – a clear shift toward token‑driven finance.

---

### WHAT HAPPENED  

**Binance.US seeks fresh capital.**  According to a CoinTelegraph report, the U.S. arm of the world’s largest crypto exchange is courting a $100 million financing round that would value the business at roughly $1 billion.  Management is said to be using the cash to rebuild market share after the 2023 settlement with U.S. regulators that forced the platform to curtail certain products and tighten compliance.  The funding round is expected to close in the next quarter, but the identity of the investors has not been disclosed.  

**Banks move deeper into digital assets.**  Deutsche Bank, Germany’s biggest lender, announced plans to extend its crypto‑custody platform from Europe into key Asian financial hubs.  The move, reported by CoinTelegraph, is driven by “the rapid growth of the digital‑asset economy” and the bank’s desire to capture institutional demand for secure, regulated storage of tokens.  The rollout is slated for early 2027, with pilot services expected in Singapore, Hong Kong and Tokyo later this year.  

**Tokenisation and after‑hours trading gain traction.**  Two separate CoinTelegraph pieces highlighted how tokenised stocks are now trading well beyond traditional market hours, effectively creating a 24‑hour “Wall Street” for equities.  The after‑hours volume, while still modest, is growing as broker‑dealers and custodians integrate blockchain‑based settlement layers that eliminate the need for a centralized clearinghouse after the close.  At the same time, the CONNECT conference in Singapore underscored that liquidity – not technology – is the next bottleneck for tokenised real‑estate and other illiquid assets.  

**Regulatory certainty solidifies for some, stays uncertain for others.**  A U.S. district judge rejected Gemini’s request to unwind its $5 million settlement with the CFTC, finding that the agency’s changed enforcement priorities and internal misconduct allegations were insufficient grounds for reversal.  The ruling, covered by CoinTelegraph, reinforces the message that past settlements remain binding unless a clear procedural error is shown.  

**Stablecoins prove cost‑effective for remittances.**  Borderless.xyz data, cited by CoinTelegraph, shows that using stablecoins for cross‑border business payments cuts fees by roughly 84 % compared with traditional wire transfers, while also settling in minutes rather than days.  The study examined a broad set of corridors, including Europe‑Asia and North‑America‑Latin America, and found that the speed advantage is most pronounced for high‑frequency, low‑value transactions.  

**The digital euro moves closer.**  European Central Bank President Christine Lagarde told reporters that the euro‑area economy remains “resilient” and that a digital euro is “imminent.”  While no launch date was given, the ECB’s technical design phase is slated to finish by mid‑2027, after which pilots with selected banks and payment providers will begin.  

**A cautionary tale of hardware tampering.**  An Asia Express‑style scammer lost $4.8 million after storing stolen crypto on Ledger devices that had been secretly altered.  The incident, reported by CoinTelegraph, illustrates that even sophisticated actors can be vulnerable to supply‑chain attacks on hardware wallets, reinforcing the need for rigorous device provenance checks.

### THE BIGGER PICTURE  

All of these developments point to the rebuilding of the **payments‑and‑settlement rail** that has powered global finance for the past half‑century.  Legacy rails—SWIFT, ACH, and traditional custodial clearinghouses—are increasingly being bypassed or supplemented by blockchain‑based layers that promise faster settlement, lower fees and programmable functionality.  Binance.US’s fundraising is a direct response to regulatory pressure that forced the platform to retreat from parts of the U.S. market; the capital infusion is aimed at rebuilding the “digital exchange” rail with stronger compliance and a broader suite of tokenised products.  Deutsche Bank’s Asian custody expansion shows that incumbent banks are no longer content to sit on the sidelines; they are building the custodial infrastructure that will sit at the base of the new rail, offering institutional clients the security and regulatory cover they need to move large tokenised balances across borders.  

**Winners** in this transition are the institutions that can combine regulatory legitimacy with blockchain efficiency.  Crypto exchanges that secure funding and tighten compliance (e.g., Binance.US) gain the liquidity needed to attract retail and institutional traders.  Global banks that launch regulated custody services (e.g., Deutsche Bank) capture a new revenue stream while providing the “bridge” that traditional corporates and treasuries require to move into digital assets.  Stablecoins, especially those backed by fiat, win by delivering dramatic cost savings on remittances, making them attractive to corporates, SMEs and diaspora communities alike.  Finally, tokenised securities and after‑hours trading platforms win by unlocking liquidity that was previously locked to the 9‑5 market, giving investors the ability to react to news in real time.  

**Losers** are the legacy service providers that cannot adapt quickly enough.  Traditional correspondent banks and wire‑transfer networks see their fee base erode as firms adopt stablecoins for the same purpose
