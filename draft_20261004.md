**HEADLINE**  
South Korea pushes token‑security rules, BNB Chain hits $1 bn in tokenized assets, while a Zano exploit and EU stable‑coin debates remind markets that speed and safety must grow together.  

---  

### WHAT HAPPENED  

**A massive minting flaw forced a Zano blockchain rollback.**  The Zano team discovered that an attacker had created more than a quadrillion “fUSD” tokens that were indistinguishable from legitimate ZANO coins. Because the forged units could not be separated on‑chain, the only remedy was to revert the entire ledger to a point before the attack, erasing the illicit supply but also wiping legitimate activity that occurred in the meantime. (CoinTelegraph)  

**South Korea is laying out a detailed rulebook for tokenized securities.**  The Financial Services Commission released a draft that sets capital‑adequacy standards for issuers, requires an over‑the‑counter (OTC) trading licence for secondary markets, and caps retail exposure to tokenized assets. The framework is designed to be in force for the 2027 rollout of the country’s broader digital‑asset strategy. (CoinTelegraph)  

**Tokenized stocks and ETFs on BNB Chain crossed the $1 bn threshold.**  The network now hosts $1.1 bn of tokenized equities and exchange‑traded funds, accounting for roughly 30 % of the $3.7 bn total market for tokenized assets across all blockchains. The growth reflects both increasing demand from institutional investors for on‑chain exposure and the network’s lower transaction costs compared with rivals. (CoinTelegraph)  

**European stable‑coin rules are under review.**  Circle, the issuer of USDC, has asked the EU to replace the MiCA requirement that stable‑coin issuers hold a minimum of 100 % of the token value in bank deposits with a more flexible liquidity‑risk framework. Circle argues that the current rule limits cross‑border issuance and raises operational costs. At the same time, European issuers are lobbying for USD‑denominated stable‑coins, saying the euro‑only offering does not meet the liquidity needs of businesses that settle internationally in dollars. (CoinTelegraph)  

**Bitcoin surged toward $87 k as short positions were liquidated.**  A wave of forced buying erased more than $120 million of short exposure, pushing the price past a key resistance level. The move highlights how thin order‑book depth can amplify price swings when leveraged traders are forced out. (CoinTelegraph)  

**Valuations in the crypto‑service sector are compressing.**  Prediction‑market platform Kalshi is seeking a $40 bn valuation, while Blockchain.com is preparing for an IPO. Both companies face pressure to justify lofty market caps that were built during the 2021‑2022 bull run, as investors now demand clearer paths to sustainable revenue. (CoinTelegraph)  

**UK finance leaders see tokenization as a game‑changer.**  A Lloyds Banking Group survey found that 71 % of senior finance executives expect tokenized assets to reshape financial services, with faster payments and settlement cited as the most valuable benefit. The result underscores growing confidence in token‑based infrastructure among traditional banks. (CoinTelegraph)  

---  

### THE BIGGER PICTURE  

All of these stories point to a single, accelerating rail: the migration of settlement, custody and liquidity from legacy, paper‑based processes to programmable, on‑chain assets.  The Zano exploit is a reminder that the rail is still being welded.  A single vulnerability can force a network to rewind, erasing trust and creating operational headaches for anyone who relied on the chain for settlement.  For banks and treasury teams, the lesson is clear – any on‑chain solution must be paired with rigorous code‑audit regimes and disaster‑recovery plans that match the expectations placed on core banking systems.  

The regulatory moves in South Korea and the EU illustrate who is trying to lay the ballast for that rail.  South Korea’s detailed capital‑requirement and licensing framework signals that regulators are willing to give tokenized securities a legal home, provided the participants meet traditional prudential standards.  The EU’s MiCA review, driven by Circle’s lobbying, shows a competing priority: preserving the flexibility that makes stable‑coins attractive for cross‑border payments while still protecting consumers.  If the EU relaxes the bank‑deposit reserve rule, issuers of USD‑stablecoins could gain a foothold in Europe, offering a de‑facto dollar bridge for corporates that currently rely on correspondent banking.  The winners would be multinational treasurers and banks that can integrate a USD‑stablecoin layer into their cash‑management platforms; the losers would be legacy correspondent banks that see their foreign‑exchange corridors eroded.  

The market data on BNB Chain and the UK survey confirm that demand is already materialising.  Tokenized equities and ETFs now represent a sizable slice of the $3.7 bn tokenized‑asset market, and a clear majority of UK finance leaders expect faster settlement to be the primary benefit.  For banks, this translates into a strategic imperative: either build or partner on a chain that can deliver low‑cost, high‑throughput token issuance and secondary trading, or risk being bypassed by corporate treasuries that can move cash instantly on‑chain.  BNB Chain’s growth suggests that ecosystems offering cheaper gas fees and strong developer support are attracting the early institutional flow.  

At the same time, the compression of crypto‑service valuations signals that the “new‑money” hype is giving way to disciplined capital allocation.  Companies like Kalshi and Blockchain.com must now prove that their business models can generate sustainable revenue beyond token‑trading fees or speculative upside.  For traditional financial institutions, this is a cue to scrutinise partnership proposals carefully: a tokenization platform with a solid, audited codebase and a clear regulatory posture may be a better long‑term ally than a high‑valuation startup that still needs to demonstrate profitability.  

Finally, Bitcoin’s price bounce underscores the volatility that still characterises the broader crypto market.  While the surge to $87 k was driven by short‑covering, it also revealed how quickly liquidity can evaporate and how leveraged positions can amplify price moves.  Treasury teams that hold Bitcoin or other crypto assets need robust risk‑limits and real‑time monitoring to avoid unintended exposure during such spikes.  

In sum, the rail being rebuilt is the settlement layer that will sit alongside, and eventually replace, many of today’s clearinghouses, custodians and correspondent banks.
