**HEADLINE**  
Circle widens lead in tokenized U.S. equities as Securitize’s market cap halves; regulators in the U.S., Canada and South Korea move closer to stable‑coin frameworks while banks and asset managers race to deploy the new infrastructure.

---

### WHAT HAPPENED  

- **Tokenized equity market reshapes** – Securitize’s on‑chain market capitalisation fell to **$208.6 million**, roughly a 50 % drop from a month ago, after the company announced a slowdown in new issuances and a migration of several of its tokenized stocks to other platforms. By contrast, Circle‑linked tokens now represent **$383.6 million** of the estimated **$3.3 billion** tokenized‑equities market, cementing Circle’s position as the dominant issuer of on‑chain share‑classes for U.S. listed companies. (CoinTelegraph)

- **Asset‑manager tokenization expands** – Aviva Investors launched a tokenized share class for its **£29 billion** sterling‑denominated fund on the **XRP Ledger**. This is the second Aviva fund to be listed on the ledger and part of a broader partnership aimed at bringing more institutional‑grade tokenized funds to the XRP ecosystem. (CoinTelegraph)

- **Regulatory moves on stablecoins** –  
  * In the United States, the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) issued its final guidance under the **GENIUS Act**, urging community banks to become “payment stablecoin issuers.” The rule, set to take effect in **January 2027**, gives banks a clear pathway to issue, hold and redeem stablecoins that are fully backed by U.S. dollars. (CoinTelegraph)  
  * Ripple secured the last piece of regulatory approval needed to operate a **national trust bank** in the U.S., allowing it to manage the reserves backing its **RLUSD** stablecoin and to provide custody services for digital assets. (CoinTelegraph)  
  * Canada opened a public consultation on a **Stablecoin Act** that mirrors the U.S. GENIUS framework, seeking input on how non‑financial institutions might issue stablecoins under a federal regime. (CoinTelegraph)  
  * South Korea’s legislature debated postponing a 2020‑draft crypto‑tax law until **2029**, citing data‑sharing concerns with U.S. authorities. The delay would keep the current tax treatment unchanged for another eight years. (CoinTelegraph)

- **Corporate finance signal** – Payments‑focused fintech RedotPay is reportedly weighing a confidential **U.S. IPO** filing before year‑end, after earlier speculation that the filing had been delayed. The move, if confirmed, would make the company one of the few pure‑play stablecoin payment processors to go public in the United States. (CoinTelegraph)

### THE BIGGER PICTURE  

**Rebuilding the securities‑distribution rail**  
The tokenized‑equities market is the modern analogue of the “railroad” that once moved paper certificates from issuers to investors. Circle’s rapid capture of market share shows that a single, well‑capitalised platform can become the de‑facto clearinghouse for on‑chain shares, especially when it offers a turnkey solution that satisfies both issuers’ compliance needs and investors’ demand for near‑instant settlement. Securitize’s decline illustrates the risk of a fragmented approach: without a robust pipeline of new issuances or a clear migration path for existing tokens, a platform can quickly lose relevance.

**Winners and losers**  
- **Winners** – Circle, Ripple, community banks that adopt stablecoin issuance, and asset managers like Aviva that already have a tokenization strategy. These players gain early‑mover advantage, access to lower‑cost settlement, and the ability to offer investors fractional ownership and 24/7 trading.  
- **Losers** – Platforms that cannot secure a critical mass of issuers (e.g., Securitize) and jurisdictions that delay clear tax or regulatory guidance (South Korea). Their participants face higher compliance costs and reduced investor confidence, which can drive capital to better‑regulated ecosystems.  

**Implications for the legacy system**  
Traditional custodians, broker‑dealers and clearing houses still dominate the bulk of equity trading, but the economics are shifting. Tokenized shares settle in minutes on public blockchains, eliminating the two‑day “T+2” lag and reducing counter‑party risk. However, the legacy system retains advantages in liquidity depth, regulatory certainty and integration with existing corporate actions processes. The emerging hybrid model—where a regulated bank issues a stablecoin, a tokenization platform creates the share‑class, and a traditional broker offers the token to retail investors—appears to be the most viable bridge for now.

**Stablecoin regulation as the new foundation**  
The OCC’s guidance and Ripple’s trust‑bank approval signal that U.S. regulators are moving from a “wait‑and‑see” stance to an operational framework that treats stablecoins as a regulated payment instrument rather than a fringe product. Canada’s consultation mirrors this trend, suggesting a coordinated North‑American approach. The net effect is a reduction in legal ambiguity for banks and fintechs, which should accelerate the launch of bank‑backed stablecoins and, by extension, the tokenized securities that rely on them for settlement currency.

### WHAT TO WATCH  

- **January 2027 – OCC stablecoin rule takes effect** – Monitor how many community banks file to become issuers and whether any start offering stablecoins directly to corporate clients.  
- **Canada’s Stablecoin Act consultation deadline (mid‑December 2026)** – Look for the government’s final policy direction and any requirements that could affect non‑bank issuers.  
- **Circle’s quarterly token issuance data (Q4 2026)** – A continued rise would reinforce its market‑lead status; a slowdown could open space for competitors.  
- **Aviva’s fund performance on the XRP Ledger (first 6 months)** – Early liquidity, redemption patterns, and investor adoption will indicate whether the XRP Ledger can support large‑scale institutional funds.  
- **RedotPay IPO filing (expected Q4 2026)** – Confirmation of a filing and the prospectus details will reveal how the market values a pure‑play stablecoin payments business and may set a valuation benchmark for similar fintechs.  

These developments together sketch the outline of a new financial infrastructure where tokenized assets, bank‑issued stablecoins, and regulated custodial services intersect. The firms that can align their technology, compliance and distribution strategies with this emerging rail are poised to capture the next wave of liquidity and client demand.
