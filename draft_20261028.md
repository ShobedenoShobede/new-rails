**HEADLINE**  
Regulators, stable‑coin rules and corporate push‑back reshape the crypto market: Binance seeks a Brazil licence, USDC eclipses USDT in euros, and tokenized funds face new hurdles.

---

**WHAT HAPPENED**  

- **Binance moves toward a Brazilian licence** – The exchange filed an application with Brazil’s central bank ahead of the Oct. 30 deadline that will determine whether it can continue operating locally. To satisfy regulators, Binance announced it will delist 22 crypto assets for Brazilian users while it negotiates the restoration of other services. (CoinTelegraph)

- **USDC becomes the dominant euro‑stablecoin** – Data from analytics firm Kaiko shows that USDC’s share of euro‑denominated stable‑coin trading rose to 56 % since January 2024. The shift follows the European Union’s Markets in Crypto‑Assets (MiCA) framework, which has forced many exchanges to limit USDT trading. (CoinTelegraph)

- **Tokenised Nasdaq‑100 fund launched on Stellar** – Asset manager Janus Henderson, together with infrastructure provider Centrifuge, introduced a blockchain‑based fund that gives eligible non‑U.S. investors exposure to the Nasdaq‑100 index. The product is issued on the Stellar network and is positioned as the latest step in Janus Henderson’s institutional tokenisation strategy. (CoinTelegraph)

- **Ripple expands RLUSD partnership with Robinhood** – Ripple and Robinhood broadened their collaboration to bring Ripple’s US‑dollar‑backed stablecoin (RLUSD) to both retail and institutional customers. The deal also deepened Ripple’s custody relationship with BNY Mellon and added a trade‑finance settlement link with fintech platform C2FO on the XRP Ledger. (CoinTelegraph)

- **Corporate resistance to tokenised stocks grows** – More than 40 % of the companies originally slated for tokenised listings on the OKX‑ICE platform have now opted out, including Intel, IBM, Oracle and IonQ. The objections highlight growing discomfort among issuers about the regulatory and reputational risk of tokenised securities. (CoinTelegraph)

- **Robinhood’s CEO warns of U.S. tokenisation uncertainty** – Vlad Tenev told investors that the United States needs more than a temporary SEC exemption for tokenised equities to thrive. He warned that the current regulatory limbo discourages “serious assets” from being tokenised in the U.S. market. (CoinTelegraph)

- **U.S. voters show appetite for fintech‑friendly candidates** – A September poll indicated that a majority of American respondents prefer political candidates who support domestic financial‑technology development, suggesting that fintech policy could become a factor in the upcoming election. (CoinTelegraph)

---

**THE BIGGER PICTURE**  

All of these stories point to a single, accelerating rail being rebuilt: the interface between traditional finance and digital‑asset ecosystems. Regulators in Brazil, the EU and the United States are tightening the rules that determine which crypto products can be offered, how they are marketed, and who can hold them. At the same time, firms that can adapt to the new compliance regime are expanding their product lines and courting institutional capital.

**Winners**  
- **Compliant stable‑coin issuers** – USDC’s surge in euro trading shows that a stable‑coin that meets MiCA’s transparency and reserve‑backing requirements can capture market share quickly when rivals are forced to scale back.  
- **Platforms that combine custody with tokenisation** – Ripple’s partnership with Robinhood and BNY Mellon demonstrates the value of pairing a regulated custodian with a blockchain settlement layer. The same logic underpins Janus Henderson’s Stellar fund, which offers investors a familiar index exposure while leveraging the speed and auditability of a public ledger.  
- **Exchanges that secure licences** – If Binance obtains its Brazilian licence, it will retain a large user base and re‑enable services that were previously suspended, giving it a competitive edge over unlicensed rivals.

**Losers**  
- **Non‑compliant stable‑coins** – USDT’s reduced visibility in euro markets illustrates how a token that does not fully align with new rules can lose liquidity and trading volume.  
- **Tokenised securities without issuer buy‑in** – The wave of opt‑outs from companies like Intel and IBM shows that tokenised equity offerings cannot succeed without clear consent from the underlying issuers. The SEC’s ambiguous stance further discourages U.S. firms from pursuing tokenised listings.  
- **Crypto platforms that rely on temporary exemptions** – Robinhood’s warning highlights the risk of building business models on short‑term regulatory relief; without a permanent framework, such ventures may struggle to attract “serious” assets.

**Old system vs. new system**  
The traditional banking and securities model has relied on centralized clearing, custodial silos and jurisdiction‑specific licences. The emerging digital‑asset model seeks to overlay those functions with blockchain‑based settlement, programmable assets and cross‑border accessibility. The current regulatory push is not a blanket rejection of innovation; rather, it is an effort to graft the new layer onto the old framework in a way that preserves consumer protection and market integrity. Companies that can bridge the two—by offering regulated custody, transparent reserves and clear legal status—are positioned to capture the flow of capital moving from legacy channels to tokenised alternatives.
