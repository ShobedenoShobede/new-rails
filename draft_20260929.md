**HEADLINE**  
EU regulator ESMA pivots MiCA agenda from rule‑making to active supervision, while institutions test new crypto‑collateral models and market players recover from recent security shocks.  

---  

**WHAT HAPPENED**  

*Regulatory shift* – In a briefing reported by **CoinTelegraph**, the chair of the European Securities and Markets Authority (ESMA) said the agency will move from drafting detailed MiCA rules to supervising how those rules are applied. The focus will be on the resilience of crypto‑asset service providers (CASPs), their outsourcing arrangements, and the practice of “reverse solicitation” – where a EU‑based client initiates contact with a non‑EU provider. ESMA also wants a harmonised reporting framework that can be used by national supervisors across the bloc.  

*Institutional collateral innovation* – **Bybit**, a major crypto exchange, announced that it will accept tokenised shares of Franklin Templeton funds – issued on the Benji protocol – as collateral for stable‑coin credit lines. Eligible institutional clients can pledge the tokenised fund shares while the underlying securities stay in a traditional off‑exchange custodian. The move is designed to give banks and treasury desks a familiar, regulated asset that can be used to obtain liquidity in the crypto market.  

*Security incident and service restoration* – **Bitget** resumed Bitcoin withdrawals after a week‑long outage caused by a $388 million hack that involved the attacker swapping stolen ETH on THORChain. The exchange said ETH withdrawals will restart on Tuesday and USDT withdrawals on Wednesday, indicating that internal controls and liquidity buffers are being rebuilt.  

*Regulatory guidance in the United States* – The U.S. **SEC** released staff guidance that mirrors recent CFTC guidance, outlining how existing federal securities laws may apply to token issuers. The guidance follows the Senate’s failure to pass the CLARITY Act, which would have created a dedicated crypto‑regulation framework. The SEC’s approach signals that token issuers should prepare for securities‑law compliance even without new legislation.  

*Capital‑market re‑entry* – **Blockchain.com** is reportedly preparing a $500 million IPO, targeting a valuation of up to $6 billion. The valuation is well below the company’s 2022 peak, reflecting the broader market’s cautious stance after a period of volatility.  

*Legal fallout in Canada* – A self‑styled “crypto king”, Aiden Pleterski, is set to represent himself at a fraud trial in Canada. He is accused of running a Ponzi‑style crypto scheme that led to a kidnapping and other criminal charges. The case underscores ongoing law‑enforcement focus on fraud in the crypto space.  

*MiCA rollout in Greece* – Greece became the first EU member to register four MiCA‑licensed providers, after its watchdog, the Hellenic Capital Market Commission (HCMC), rejected a claim that Binance’s founder had made misleading statements. The registration marks the first practical step of the MiCA regime in an EU country.  

---  

**THE BIGGER PICTURE**  

The EU’s pivot from drafting rules to supervising compliance is the most visible sign that the crypto‑regulatory “rail” is being rebuilt from a theoretical framework into an operational one. By moving to supervision, ESMA is acknowledging that the market has matured enough to enforce standards rather than merely prescribe them. The emphasis on CASP resilience, outsourcing, and reverse solicitation directly addresses the weaknesses exposed by recent hacks and cross‑border service models.  

Who wins? Institutions that can demonstrate robust governance and transparent reporting will gain access to the EU market on a level playing field. The Bybit‑Franklin Templeton partnership is a case in point: it offers banks a regulated, tokenised asset that satisfies both traditional custody requirements and the demand for crypto‑linked liquidity. For banks and treasury teams, this creates a new source of short‑term funding without exposing them to the custody risks that have plagued pure‑crypto exchanges.  

Who loses? Pure‑play crypto exchanges that rely on opaque governance or that have not yet built out comprehensive outsourcing controls may find themselves barred from EU customers. Bitget’s recent hack illustrates the operational risk premium that regulators are now willing to impose. Even though the exchange is restoring withdrawals, the incident will likely be a data point in ESMA’s future supervisory reviews, potentially leading to higher capital or insurance requirements for similar platforms.  

The shift also reshapes the old system of fragmented national supervision into a more centralized, EU‑wide oversight model. In the United States, the SEC’s alignment with the CFTC signals a parallel trend: regulators are converging on a common set of expectations for token issuers, even in the absence of dedicated legislation. This reduces regulatory arbitrage, forcing issuers to adopt securities‑law compliance as a baseline.  

The market’s capital‑raising activity, as shown by Blockchain.com’s IPO plans, suggests that investors are cautiously returning to crypto‑related equities, but only at valuations that reflect realistic earnings and risk profiles. The lower target valuation compared with the 2022 peak indicates that the “boom‑bust” cycle is being tempered by a more disciplined investor base.  

Finally, the legal proceedings in Canada and the first MiCA registrations in Greece illustrate that enforcement and compliance are moving from the periphery to the core of the ecosystem. Fraud prosecutions send a deterrent signal, while formal licensing under MiCA creates a clear pathway for legitimate providers to operate across the EU. Together, these developments tighten the gap between the legacy financial system and the emerging crypto‑enabled services, rewarding firms that can bridge the two worlds with compliant, well‑governed products.

**WHAT TO WATCH**  

- **ESMA supervisory framework rollout** – Expect detailed supervisory guidelines and reporting templates from ESMA within the next 60 days. Their release will clarify the data and governance metrics that CASPs must submit to national regulators.  

- **Bybit‑Franklin Templeton pilot performance** – Monitor the first three months of collateral usage (through Q4 2026). Key metrics will be the volume of stable‑coin credit lines generated and any regulatory feedback from EU supervisors.  

- **Bitget liquidity and security upgrades** – Track Bitget’s ability to meet its scheduled ETH and USDT withdrawal windows. Any further delays could trigger supervisory scrutiny under the upcoming ESMA supervision regime.  

- **SEC‑CFTC guidance impact on token issuers** – Watch for SEC enforcement actions or “no‑action” letters referencing the new staff guidance, especially for token sales scheduled in 2027.  

- **Blockchain.com IPO timing and pricing** – The company is expected to file its S‑1 in early Q1 2027. The pricing range and investor demand will be an early barometer of how capital markets value crypto‑infrastructure firms after the recent market correction.  

These items will shape the risk‑reward calculus for banks, treasury desks, and institutional investors as the regulatory environment solidifies and the market tests new institutional‑grade crypto products.
