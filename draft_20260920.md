**Headline**  
European banks double their MiCA presence while U.S. regulators push a new crypto‑market framework; at the same time, a self‑custodial “super‑app” and a Japan‑backed stable‑coin payment platform expand the commercial use‑case landscape.

---

### What Happened  

In the past 24 hours a cluster of developments signaled that both the supply‑side and the regulatory side of crypto are moving from experiment to mainstream.  

* **World Money** – the fintech arm of the World ecosystem launched a self‑custodial “super‑app” that bundles stable‑coin payments, digital‑asset rewards and spot‑trading. The service is now live in more than 150 jurisdictions, positioning the app as a one‑stop shop for retail users who want to hold and move crypto without a custodial intermediary. (CoinTelegraph)

* **Binance** – the exchange rebuffed media reports that the European Central Bank had intervened in its Greek MiCA (Markets in Crypto‑Assets) licence request. Binance reiterated its commitment to obtaining a full EU authorisation, signalling that it still sees Europe as a key growth market. (CoinTelegraph)

* **dtcpay** – Japan’s SBI Group led a $25 million Series A round for the stable‑coin payment processor. The capital will fund the expansion of its merchant network and the rollout of new payment products, reinforcing SBI’s strategy of integrating crypto‑based settlement into its broader financial services platform. (CoinTelegraph)

* **Hyperliquid’s HYPE token** – after the platform introduced a manual borrowing feature that lets users borrow stable‑coins using HYPE or Bitcoin as collateral, the token hit an all‑time high of $90.92. The move illustrates how DeFi‑style lending tools are being layered onto centralized exchange products to attract yield‑seeking traders. (CoinTelegraph)

* **Bank participation in MiCA** – the European Securities and Markets Authority (ESMA) reported that banks now account for 23 % of the entities listed on the EU MiCA register, roughly double their share since late June. This reflects a rapid onboarding of traditional financial institutions into the regulated crypto‑service space. (CoinTelegraph)

* **Bitcoin price** – BTC surged to $81 000 as U.S. 30‑year Treasury yields rose again amid renewed oil‑price volatility. The price move underscores the continued correlation between macro‑risk assets and crypto’s risk‑on perception. (CoinTelegraph)

* **U.S. regulatory push** – the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) submitted a comprehensive crypto‑market regulatory plan to the White House for review, days after the Senate failed to advance the CLARITY Act. The filing outlines how the CFTC intends to use existing authority to supervise derivatives, spot trading and custodial activities. (CoinTelegraph)

* **Crypto‑linked equities** – stocks of firms such as Coinbase and Strategy share rallied after the regulatory news, suggesting that market participants view the CFTC’s plan as a stabilising signal compared with the uncertainty created by the stalled CLARITY legislation. (CoinTelegraph)

### The Bigger Picture  

All of these headlines point to a single, overarching rail being rebuilt: the **interface between regulated finance and decentralized digital assets**.  

1. **Who is building the rail?**  
   *Traditional banks* are moving from peripheral exposure to core service providers. Their doubling presence on the MiCA register shows they are seeking licences that will let them offer custodial, settlement and lending services under a clear EU framework. In parallel, *large crypto exchanges* (Binance) are seeking the same licences to avoid a fragmented regulatory landscape. On the demand side, *self‑custodial apps* like World Money and *stable‑coin payment platforms* such as dtcpay are creating consumer‑grade products that can be integrated into existing banking channels, especially in markets where fiat‑to‑crypto conversion is still friction‑heavy.

2. **Who wins?**  
   - **Institutions that can combine compliance with product innovation**. Banks that secure MiCA authorisations will be able to monetize crypto custody, cross‑border payments and on‑ramp services while retaining their risk‑management frameworks.  
   - **Technology firms that embed crypto into everyday workflows**. World Money’s “super‑app” model, which eliminates the need for a third‑party custodian, offers a low‑cost, high‑velocity channel for retail adoption. Its global rollout gives it a first‑mover advantage in markets where regulatory clarity is still emerging.  
   - **Investors seeking yield**. The launch of manual borrowing on Hyperliquid and the record HYPE price demonstrate that leveraged, DeFi‑style products can thrive when offered on regulated or semi‑regulated exchanges, attracting capital that would otherwise stay in traditional fixed‑income markets.

3. **Who loses?**  
   - **Unlicensed or “shadow” service providers** that cannot meet MiCA or forthcoming U.S. standards. Their market share will shrink as banks and licensed exchanges win corporate and retail contracts.  
   - **Speculative traders relying on regulatory arbitrage**. The CFTC’s plan and the resurgence of crypto equities suggest that regulators are closing loopholes that previously allowed high‑leverage, low‑margin trading with limited oversight.  

4. **Old system vs. new system**  
   The legacy banking model treats crypto as a peripheral asset class, often relegated to a “sandbox” or a separate subsidiary. The emerging system integrates crypto directly into the core banking stack: custodial licences, AML/KYC compliance, and settlement infrastructure become part of the same regulatory perimeter that governs FX and securities. This integration reduces friction for corporate treasury departments that want to pay suppliers in stable‑coins, and for institutional investors seeking exposure without a separate custodial chain. At the same time, the rise of self‑custodial apps introduces a parallel path where end‑users retain private‑key control, but still interact with regulated on‑ramps and off‑ramps, blurring the line between “banked” and “unbanked” crypto usage.
