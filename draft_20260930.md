**HEADLINE**  
EU’s MiCA rollout gains first entrants, US regulator vacancies widen oversight gap, while tokenisation and AI‑driven digital‑currency pilots signal a shift toward institutional‑grade crypto services.  

---  

### WHAT HAPPENED  

In the past 24 hours a cluster of developments underscored the accelerating convergence of traditional finance, public‑sector policy and crypto‑native innovation.  

* **Greece becomes the first EU member to register crypto‑service providers under the Markets in Crypto‑Assets (MiCA) framework.** The Hellenic Capital Market Commission (HCMC) added four firms to the EU‑wide MiCA register, marking the first operational entry of the regulation that the European Commission rolled out in 2024. The HCMC also rejected a claim that a Binance executive had made remarks to the regulator, reinforcing the commission’s stance on transparent disclosure (CoinTelegraph).  

* **Bitcoin rallied to US $84,000 after the 30‑year US Treasury yield hit a 24‑year high.** The price bounce came as the long‑dated benchmark climbed above 4.5 %, a level that traditionally pressures risk assets. Analysts noted that Bitcoin held a “trend‑defining” support zone, suggesting the market may be testing the resilience of crypto as a hedge against rising sovereign‑debt costs (CoinTelegraph).  

* **The European Central Bank (ECB) put AI‑driven payment agents on the agenda for its upcoming digital‑euro pilot.** The central bank issued a request for proposals from private firms to explore machine‑to‑machine interactions and autonomous agents that could execute payments on behalf of users. The pilot, slated for 2027, aims to embed algorithmic decision‑making into the digital‑euro ecosystem (CoinTelegraph).  

* **Kakaopay Securities announced a partnership with Dinari and Ondo Finance to tokenise Korean‑listed equities.** The collaboration will create blockchain‑based representations of domestic stocks, enabling cross‑border investors to access Korean equities without the friction of traditional custodial and settlement infrastructure (CoinTelegraph).  

* **OpenAI is reportedly courting a $30 billion funding round that could push its valuation to $1.4 trillion.** The AI‑lab’s capital raise, if completed, would precede a public‑market debut planned for 2027, underscoring the growing appetite of institutional investors for large‑scale AI and data‑centric businesses (CoinTelegraph).  

* **US regulatory bodies face a leadership vacuum.** A recent resignation left the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) and the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) each with only three sitting commissioners, a historic low for agencies that oversee a $3 trillion crypto market. The reduced quorum could delay rule‑making and enforcement actions (CoinTelegraph).  

* **Illinois released draft crypto‑tax regulations that clarify the treatment of DeFi protocols, stablecoins, bridges and self‑custody transfers.** The state proposes a 0.2 % transaction tax on digital‑asset trades, with detailed guidance on how the levy applies across the emerging ecosystem (CoinTelegraph).  

Collectively, these items illustrate a moment where policy, market pricing, and product innovation intersect, each influencing the trajectory of crypto’s integration with mainstream finance.  

---  

### THE BIGGER PICTURE  

**Which rail is being rebuilt?**  
The “rail” being reconstructed is the regulatory‑compliant infrastructure that enables large‑scale, cross‑border financial flows. MiCA’s first registrations, the ECB’s AI‑agent pilot, and Illinois’ granular tax rules all signal a move from a fragmented, “wild‑west” environment toward a standardized, institution‑ready track. Simultaneously, tokenisation projects like Kakaopay’s effort to digitise Korean equities are laying the physical‑layer tracks—digital assets that can travel on the new rail without legacy settlement bottlenecks.  

**Who wins?**  
* **Institutional investors and treasury teams** gain clearer legal certainty and a broader menu of crypto‑adjacent products. The MiCA register gives banks a vetted list of service providers, while the digital‑euro AI pilot promises faster, programmable payments that could be embedded in corporate cash‑management platforms.  
* **Fintech firms that can bridge regulated finance and blockchain** stand to capture market share. Dinari, Ondo and Kakaopay’s tokenisation partnership illustrates how a compliant token layer can unlock liquidity for otherwise ill‑iquid equities, appealing to global asset managers seeking diversified exposure.  
* **Technology providers and AI developers** such as OpenAI benefit from the influx of capital that validates large‑scale AI as a strategic asset for financial services, potentially feeding into smarter compliance tools, risk models and client‑facing chat interfaces.  

**Who loses?**  
* **Unregulated crypto exchanges and service providers** that cannot meet MiCA’s stringent capital, governance and consumer‑protection requirements may see market share erode as banks gravitate toward compliant partners.  
* **Smaller DeFi protocols** could feel the impact of Illinois’ transaction tax and the broader trend of jurisdictional taxation, which adds a cost layer that may deter retail participation and reduce network effects.  
* **Regulatory oversight capacity** is temporarily weakened in the United States due to the commissioner vacancies, creating a short‑term risk of regulatory arbitrage for firms that can exploit the slower rule‑making cadence.  

**What does this mean for the old system vs. the new one?**  
The old system—characterised by siloed clearing houses, paper‑based settlement and a patchwork of national crypto rules—is being supplanted by a hybrid model where sovereign digital currencies, tokenised securities and AI‑mediated payments coexist on interoperable blockchains. The shift is not a wholesale replacement; rather, it is an overlay that adds speed, programmability and cross‑border reach to existing processes. The price bounce in Bitcoin, driven by macro‑bond yields, shows that crypto still reacts to traditional financial signals, but the growing infrastructure for compliance and tokenisation suggests that price movements will increasingly reflect fundamentals tied to real‑world assets and institutional demand, not just speculative sentiment.
