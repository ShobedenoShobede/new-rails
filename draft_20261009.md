**Headline**  
EU regulators give crypto firms three‑month deadline to drop non‑MiCA stablecoins as tokenisation gains institutional traction and regulators flag new systemic risks  

---

### What Happened  

- **EU stablecoin crackdown** – The European Securities and Markets Authority (ESMA) announced that all crypto‑service providers operating in the EU must cease offering or supporting stablecoins that do not meet the requirements of the Markets in Crypto‑Assets Regulation (MiCA). Firms have three months to unwind existing positions and to certify any remaining stablecoins against the new attestation framework. (CoinTelegraph)

- **Institutional Bitcoin finance moves forward** – Sui’s Layer‑1 network is preparing to launch its “Hashi” protocol with more than **$500 million** of pre‑committed financing from institutional investors. The rollout includes a partnership with Anchorage, which will provide settlement and self‑custody services for Bitcoin‑backed products. (CoinTelegraph)

- **Policy‑level push on crypto and anti‑corruption** – The European Parliament’s Committee on Economic and Monetary Affairs has placed crypto assets on its anti‑corruption agenda, urging tighter asset‑recovery tools and calling for the European Commission to embed crypto‑specific measures in its upcoming anti‑corruption strategy, due by year‑end. (CoinTelegraph)

- **Fidelity’s “no‑going‑back” stance** – Fidelity’s head of digital‑asset strategy told investors that tokenisation offers “structural advantages” for banks and asset managers, citing lower settlement costs, fractional ownership and 24/7 market access as reasons why traditional institutions will increasingly move on‑chain. (CoinTelegraph)

- **US government moves seized Bitcoin** – Blockchain analysts traced a US Treasury‑controlled wallet that transferred roughly **$1 billion** of Bitcoin, the proceeds of the 2022 Bitfinex hack, in a single transaction. The move signals that authorities are consolidating and potentially preparing to liquidate or redeploy the assets. (CoinTelegraph)

- **IMF warns of tokenised market risks** – The International Monetary Fund released a study warning that tokenised equity markets have shown lower liquidity and higher price volatility than conventional exchanges, even as demand for round‑the‑clock trading rises. (CoinTelegraph)

- **Securitize launches tokenised US equities on Solana** – Securitize’s new platform now lists twelve US stocks as blockchain‑based tokens on the Solana network, granting eligible investors dividend and voting rights and enabling 24/7 trading. The announcement sent the company’s shares up more than 10 %. (CoinTelegraph)

- **Asia’s divergent trends** – Despite a ban on crypto services, China’s peer‑to‑peer (P2P) stablecoin wallets grew **43‑fold** in the past year, while South Korea’s crypto economy reached **$450 billion**, making it the largest in East Asia. (CoinTelegraph)

---

### The Bigger Picture  

The EU’s three‑month deadline marks the first large‑scale enforcement of MiCA, the world’s most comprehensive crypto‑asset rulebook. By forcing firms to purge non‑compliant stablecoins, regulators are building a “clean‑sheet” foundation for future on‑chain finance. The move effectively **rebuilds the rail** that once carried fiat‑backed, centrally‑issued stablecoins into the payments ecosystem, replacing it with a lane that only admits assets vetted for reserve transparency, governance and consumer protection.  

**Winners** in this reshaping are the platforms that can meet MiCA’s attestations and the custodians that already hold audited reserves. Companies like **Anchorage** and **Securitize**, which combine compliance infrastructure with token‑issuance capabilities, stand to capture a larger share of institutional demand. Fidelity’s public endorsement signals that large asset managers are preparing to allocate capital to tokenised securities, a shift that will increase the volume of on‑chain settlement and drive demand for regulated custodial services.  

**Losers** are the “wild‑west” stablecoins that lack transparent backing or independent audits. Those projects will either need to overhaul their reserve models or exit the EU market entirely. Traditional clearing houses and custodians that rely on legacy settlement cycles may also see a gradual erosion of their moat as tokenised assets enable near‑instant finality and fractional ownership, undercutting the value of batch‑processing and high‑margin clearing fees.  

The **IMF’s cautionary note** adds a counterweight to the optimism. Tokenised equity markets have demonstrated lower depth and higher price swings, a symptom of fragmented liquidity across multiple blockchain venues. If regulators do not address cross‑chain market‑making and enforce robust disclosure, the volatility could spill over into broader financial stability metrics, especially as banks begin to hold tokenised assets on balance sheets.  

The **US government’s Bitcoin consolidation** underscores how sovereign actors are becoming active participants in the crypto ecosystem. By moving a $1 billion stash in a single transaction, the Treasury signals both operational competence and an intention to treat seized crypto as a fungible, liquid asset class—potentially setting a precedent for future asset‑recovery operations.  

Finally, the **Asian data points** illustrate that regulatory pressure does not uniformly suppress crypto activity. China’s P2P stablecoin surge shows that demand for dollar‑linked digital cash persists, albeit in informal channels, while South Korea’s massive crypto economy demonstrates how a permissive regulatory environment can nurture a thriving market. For global institutions, these divergent trajectories present both risk (exposure to unregulated P2P networks) and opportunity (large, liquid pools of capital ready for tokenised products).  

In sum, the rail being rebuilt is the **regulatory‑compliant, token‑first infrastructure** that will connect traditional finance to blockchain‑based assets. Institutions that can navigate MiCA, provide audited custodial services, and offer tokenised securities with clear voting and dividend rights are positioned to capture the next wave of on‑chain capital. Those clinging to opaque stablecoins or legacy settlement models risk being left behind or forced to re‑engineer their operations under stricter supervision.
