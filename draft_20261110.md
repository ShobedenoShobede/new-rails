**HEADLINE**  
EU launches a standards‑setting group for tokenised securities as banks, exchanges and crypto firms race to embed stable‑coin payments and 24/7 trading platforms.

---

### WHAT HAPPENED  

Over the past 24 hours a cluster of announcements has highlighted how the traditional financial ecosystem is tightening its grip on crypto‑based infrastructure.  

* The European Union has opened applications for an industry‑led “Tokenised Securities Standards Group.”  The group, to be formed by the end of November, is tasked with delivering a set of technical and operational standards for the custody, settlement and reporting of tokenised securities, with a target rollout by early 2028 (CoinTelegraph, 1 Nov).  The EU’s move follows the MiCA framework and signals a shift from high‑level regulation to concrete implementation rules that will be used by custodians, clearing houses and market operators across the bloc.  

* In the United Kingdom, the fallout from the $2.85 bn Binance hack has reignited debate over stable‑coin “freeze” powers.  An opinion piece argues that the ability to freeze a stable‑coin after a breach proved valuable, and that the UK regulator should make such a freeze a prerequisite for any stable‑coin to be listed on a UK exchange (CoinTelegraph, 1 Nov).  The proposal would give the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) a direct lever to intervene in on‑chain assets, a tool that has so far been limited to off‑chain custodial arrangements.  

* On the corporate‑bank side, PNC Bank announced a pilot that lets eligible corporate and institutional clients receive USDC on Ethereum and automatically convert each token into a US‑dollar deposit at the end of the transaction (CoinTelegraph, 1 Nov).  The service is positioned as a bridge between the speed of blockchain payments and the accounting certainty of traditional fiat deposits.  

* In the exchange arena, two parallel developments point to a growing appetite for 24/7, token‑based equity trading.  The New York Stock Exchange has selected Avalanche’s AVAX blockchain as the technical foundation for a forthcoming tokenised stock‑trading platform, sending AVAX up roughly 10 % on the day of the announcement (CoinTelegraph, 1 Nov).  Meanwhile, Formation Securities filed with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission for an exemption that would allow it to launch a Solana‑based venue offering continuous trading of tokenised shares from firms such as Galaxy Digital, Exodus and Forward Industries, with a target launch in early 2027 (CoinTelegraph, 1 Nov).  

* Finally, the market’s risk side is also in focus.  Elliptic traced a series of six new “memecoins” on the NEAR protocol that were used by North Korean hackers to launder proceeds from the Binance breach, illustrating how token creation on emerging chains can be weaponised for illicit finance (CoinTelegraph, 1 Nov).  At the same time, crypto‑custody provider BitGo reported that a $71 m unrealised gain on its crypto holdings nearly doubled Q3 net income, even as revenue fell 51 % (CoinTelegraph, 1 Nov), underscoring the volatility of crypto‑linked balance‑sheet items for traditional‑finance‑adjacent firms.

---

### THE BIGGER PICTURE  

All of these stories are different strands of the same transformation: the “rail” that once carried only paper‑based securities, cash settlements and batch‑processed trades is being rebuilt as a real‑time, token‑enabled network that can be accessed by banks, custodians, exchanges and corporate treasuries alike.  

**Who wins?**  
* **Banks and custodians that can embed token infrastructure** – PNC’s USDC‑to‑deposit pilot shows that banks can monetize blockchain speed while preserving the accounting certainty that regulators demand.  Those that master the “on‑ramp/off‑ramp” process will capture new corporate‑treasury cash‑management business and deepen relationships with fintech partners.  
* **Infrastructure providers that set the rules** – The EU standards group will become the de‑facto reference for tokenised securities across Europe.  Companies that contribute to, or align early with, those standards (e.g., custodians, DLT‑platform vendors, clearing houses) will gain a competitive edge when the rules become mandatory.  
* **Exchange operators that adopt high‑throughput chains** – NYSE’s partnership with Avalanche and Formation Securities’ Solana venue illustrate that traditional market operators are willing to move to layer‑1 blockchains that promise sub‑second settlement and 24/7 trading.  Early adopters can offer differentiated products—continuous equity markets, fractional ownership, cross‑border settlement—that are difficult to deliver on legacy infrastructure.  

**Who loses?**  
* **Legacy settlement and clearing firms that cling to batch processing** – Their cost structures and latency will become increasingly unattractive compared with on‑chain settlement that can settle in seconds.  Without a clear roadmap to integrate with the emerging standards, they risk being bypassed by banks and corporates seeking faster cash‑flow.  
* **Stable‑coin issuers that ignore regulatory expectations** – The UK debate on mandatory freeze powers signals that regulators will not tolerate “unfreezable” tokens when large‑scale theft occurs.  Issuers that cannot demonstrate a robust, regulator‑approved freeze mechanism may be barred from listed markets, limiting their liquidity and adoption.  
* **Crypto projects that prioritize anonymity over compliance** – The NEAR‑based memecoin laundering scheme shows that new chains can become havens for illicit activity.  As regulators tighten AML expectations for token creation, projects that cannot prove on‑chain traceability may see their ecosystems shrink or be forced to adopt costly compliance layers.  

**What does this mean for the old system vs. the new one?**  
The old system—centralised custodians, overnight settlement, and a clear separation between cash and securities—relied on trust in intermediaries and the friction of batch processing.  The emerging token‑based rail collapses that friction by allowing the same asset to exist simultaneously as a digital token and a ledger entry, with settlement that can be finalised in minutes.  However, the shift is not a wholesale replacement; rather, it is a hybrid model where banks act as “trusted gateways” that convert on‑chain tokens into regulated fiat deposits (as PNC demonstrates) and where regulators impose standards that preserve legal certainty (the EU group, UK freeze rules).  The net effect is a re‑allocation of value from pure custodial fees to service‑layer fees—API integration, compliance tooling, and real‑time liquidity management.

### WHAT TO WATCH  

- **EU standards finalisation timeline** – The group aims to publish a draft by early 2028.  Watch for the first public consultation (expected Q1 2027) and the EU’s formal endorsement, which will likely trigger a wave of compliance projects across Europe.  
- **UK FCA guidance on stable‑coin freezes** – The FCA is expected to issue a consultation paper in the next 60 days.  The outcome will set a precedent for other jurisdictions and could affect listings on the London Stock Exchange and other UK‑based platforms.  
- **NYSE‑Avalanche settlement pilot rollout** – NYSE has indicated a “planned tokenised stock trading platform” but no launch date.  A beta launch in H1 2027 would give a concrete benchmark for latency, cost and regulatory clearance.  
- **Formation Securities Solana venue filing outcome** – The SEC’s decision on the exemption request is due by the end of 2026.  Approval would make the first 24/7, token‑based equity market in the U.S., prompting other exchanges to file similar requests.
