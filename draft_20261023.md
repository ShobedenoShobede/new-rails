**HEADLINE**  
UK’s upcoming crypto rules threaten regulated DeFi access, while U.S. banks and data providers race to embed tokenised deposits and real‑time on‑chain data into mainstream finance.

---

### WHAT HAPPENED  

* **Regulatory friction in the UK** – In an opinion piece for *CoinTelegraph*, Katie Harries, head of European policy at Coinbase, warned that the draft 2027 UK crypto framework could effectively bar regulated firms from offering DeFi services. The draft treats “access to DeFi protocols” as a “high‑risk activity” and would require firms to obtain a separate licence or cease the service altogether. Harries argues the approach undermines the UK’s ambition to become a hub for responsible innovation and could push DeFi‑oriented capital to more permissive jurisdictions. (CoinTelegraph, “The UK needs a workable route to regulated DeFi access”).

* **Tokenised deposits move into the banking mainstream** – SoFi Bank announced the launch of **SoFi Earn**, a bank‑issued tokenised deposit that pays a 4.40 % annual percentage yield (APY) and is covered by FDIC insurance. The product lets customers move the deposit into stablecoins at any time, creating a bridge between traditional savings accounts and crypto‑based yield products. The token is built on a private‑ledger infrastructure that mirrors the underlying deposit, giving custodial banks a way to offer crypto‑compatible cash management without exposing themselves to un‑insured risk. (CoinTelegraph, “SoFi unveils bank‑issued tokenised deposit for yield‑bearing crypto funds”).

* **Institutional mining resurfaces** – CryptoQuant’s CEO, Ki Young‑Ju, highlighted a steady rise in Bitcoin’s hash‑rate, attributing the rebound to public miners backed by large traditional finance (TradFi) players re‑entering the market. The increase in computational power, he says, is an “indirect bullish signal” that institutional money is again willing to fund the energy‑intensive mining ecosystem, improving network security and potentially stabilising Bitcoin’s price dynamics. (CoinTelegraph, “CryptoQuant CEO calls rising Bitcoin hashrate ‘indirect bullish signal’”).

* **On‑chain data becomes real‑time for institutions** – Analytics platform Dune launched a suite of real‑time data feeds covering more than 200 on‑chain trading venues. The service is designed to integrate directly with enterprise‑grade systems, giving treasurers, risk managers, and portfolio analysts the same latency and reliability they expect from traditional market data vendors. Dune’s move reflects growing demand from banks and asset managers to incorporate tokenised securities and decentralized exchange activity into their existing reporting and compliance frameworks. (CoinTelegraph, “Dune launches real‑time data feeds covering 200 onchain trading venues”).

* **Legal turbulence for stablecoin incumbents** – A Circle shareholder filed a lawsuit alleging that former President Donald Trump, his firm World Liberty Financial, and real‑estate investor Steve Witkoff engaged in “corrupt” dealings that left Circle unable to compete with rival stablecoins. The complaint claims the alleged actions caused a loss of confidence in USDC, potentially harming the broader stablecoin market that many institutions rely on for settlement and liquidity. (CoinTelegraph, “Circle shareholder sues Trump, World Liberty and Steve Witkoff over crypto deals”).

* **Ethereum testnet delay signals heightened security focus** – The Ethereum Foundation postponed the launch of the Glamsterdam “Hoodi” testnet after ChainSecurity identified 13 smart‑contract patterns that could be adversely affected by the planned gas‑price changes. The delay underscores the ecosystem’s increasing emphasis on pre‑emptive risk management, especially as more institutional capital moves onto layer‑2 solutions that rely on predictable gas economics. (CoinTelegraph, “Ethereum delays Glamsterdam Hoodi testnet launch over security risks”).

### THE BIGGER PICTURE  

**Rebuilding the “access” rail** – The UK story reveals a fundamental clash between two emerging rails: the *regulated financial‑services rail* (banks, broker‑dealers, custodians) and the *open‑protocol DeFi rail* (smart contracts, permissionless liquidity). By treating DeFi access as a high‑risk activity that requires a separate licence, regulators risk severing the bridge that allows the traditional sector to tap the efficiency and composability of decentralized finance. The winners of a permissive approach are the fintech firms that can bundle compliance with on‑chain liquidity, and the broader economy that gains cheaper, 24/7 cash‑management tools. The losers are legacy banks that stay locked in siloed, batch‑processed systems and the UK’s ambition to host a vibrant crypto‑innovation hub.

**Tokenised deposits as the “gateway” product** – SoFi’s FDIC‑insured tokenised deposit illustrates a practical way to embed crypto‑compatible assets into the existing banking stack. By issuing a digital representation of a regulated deposit, SoFi gives corporate treasurers and retail savers a familiar, insured product that can be instantly moved into stablecoins for yield or cross‑border payments. The model shows how banks can capture crypto‑related revenue without exposing themselves to the uninsured risks of pure‑crypto products. The beneficiaries are banks that adopt the token‑as‑a‑service model and the institutional investors that need a low‑friction cash‑equivalent on‑ramps. The downside falls on pure‑play crypto lenders that lack regulatory backing and on jurisdictions that continue to treat tokenised deposits as “unregulated securities”.

**Institutional mining and network security** – The resurgence of hash‑rate, driven by TradFi‑backed miners, signals that the Bitcoin network is re‑aligning with the risk‑management expectations of large investors. Higher hash‑rate reduces the probability of a 51 % attack and improves block‑finality, making Bitcoin a more reliable collateral asset for treasury‑level balance‑sheet management. The “indirect bullish signal” is less about price and more about the network’s resilience, which directly affects the valuation models that banks use for Bitcoin‑backed credit facilities. The winners are banks that can now price Bitcoin exposure with a tighter risk premium; the losers are entities that have built business cases on low‑cost, low‑security mining operations.

**Data pipelines closing the on‑chain/off‑chain gap** – Dune’s real‑time feeds are a direct response to the “data vacuum” that has long limited institutional adoption of tokenised assets. By delivering low‑latency, audited on‑chain market data through APIs that integrate with existing treasury management systems (TMS) and risk engines, Dune removes a key barrier to the inclusion of decentralized exchange (DEX) activity in regulatory reporting, stress testing, and liquidity management. The rail being rebuilt here is the *information layer* that connects traditional finance’s batch‑oriented data feeds to the continuous, granular flow of blockchain activity. Banks that adopt these feeds can enhance their market‑risk models and meet upcoming ESG and transparency mandates. Those that ignore the shift risk falling behind on compliance and missing out on new yield sources.

**Legal risk for stablecoins** – The Circle lawsuit underscores that even well‑capitalised stablecoin issuers are vulnerable to political and reputational shocks. If the case proceeds and uncovers material wrongdoing, it could trigger tighter U.S. oversight of stablecoin reserves, forcing issuers to hold higher‑quality collateral or to obtain banking licences. The rail being tested is the *stablecoin‑to‑bank* conduit that underpins many corporate cash‑management strategies. Winners would be banks that secure a custodial role for stablecoins under a clear regulatory framework; losers would be issuers that cannot prove the integrity of their reserve management.
